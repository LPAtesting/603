Q13 · Only Rawls can justify laws in a pluralist democracy

Paper 4, Section A · This year's statement style · question 13 of 60

“A theory that claims to know what is objectively good for human beings is unfit for a pluralist democracy. Finnis’s natural law, with its list of basic goods and its absolute prohibitions, would have Parliament dictate how each citizen must live, whereas Rawls’s political liberalism leaves every citizen free to pursue his own conception of the good. Only Rawls’s theory can therefore justify laws that bind religious and secular citizens alike.”

Critically evaluate and comment on this statement by reference to the theories of Finnis and Rawls covered in this course, and any relevant illustrations.

Pick 3

Why these three

What the question is testing. The statement caricatures both theorists. The hidden tension: sentence 2 says Rawls leaves every citizen free, and sentence 3 praises him for laws that bind.

Sentence 2 on Finnis, "dictate how each citizen must live": 5.5. His common good is a set of conditions, and a state has no single life-plan. Concede that he is less liberal than he looks: the state may favour the basic goods.

Sentence 2 on Rawls, "leaves every citizen free": 6.2. Rawls's freedom is moral and conditioned on being reasonable, the same exclusion charged against Finnis. Finnis's reply is that the thin theory is arbitrary.

Sentence 3, "religious and secular citizens alike": 6.4. Rawls sorts reasons by kind, Finnis and TSH by merit. So the lines blur, and the concession is that merit settles truth, not coercion.

The close ones. 5.1 shows the goods have no ranking, 6.7 shows both protect religion, and 6.1 gives Rawls's test.

Verdict line (from the answer guide)

The statement caricatures both: Finnis's state secures conditions rather than dictating lives, and Rawls's neutrality is itself moral and excludes the "unreasonable"; pluralism needs reasons accessible to all, sound on the merits, and both theories supply part of that test.
