Q60 · A law binds because it coordinates, whatever its content

Paper 15, Section B · This year's statement style · question 60 of 60

“A law binds because it coordinates. Like the rule that we drive on the left, its value lies in everyone following the same rule, not in what the rule says. So long as Parliament’s laws are clear, public and stable, it hardly matters what they require, and citizens owe them the same obedience whatever their content.”

Critically evaluate and comment on this statement by reference to the theories of Finnis, Fuller, Hart and Radbruch covered in this course, and any relevant illustrations.

Pick 3

Why these three

What the question is testing. Finnis, Fuller, Hart and Radbruch on why law binds. The hidden tension: the last sentence drops the condition that makes coordination valuable. Coordinating a pogrom is coordination too.

Sentences 1 and 2, "drive on the left": 12.1. A driving rule is a determinatio, a choice among morally acceptable options. Its content does not matter only because every option is acceptable. Natural law still condemns vesting authority in bandits.

"Clear, public and stable": 3.3. Those are Fuller's requirements, and they bind through reciprocity and respect for agency. But legality is thin, so a regime can meet all eight and still be wicked.

"The same obedience whatever their content": 8.7. The sentence merges three questions. Validity does not settle the moral duty, and fair play, not coordination alone, is the common moral reason to obey.

The close ones. 8.8 gives Radbruch's tiers, 3.7 gives reciprocity, and 5.5 gives Finnis on the common good.

Verdict line (from the answer guide)

Coordination explains why many laws bind regardless of which option they pick, but only among acceptable options; content matters as soon as a law chooses against justice, so obedience is owed to clear, public, stable laws only below the threshold of intolerable injustice.
