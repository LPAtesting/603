Q32 · Rawls's supreme court should settle divisive questions

Paper 8, Section B · This year's statement style · question 32 of 60

“Rawls held up the supreme court as the exemplar of public reason. Judges, unlike legislators, must give reasons that every citizen can accept, and they are insulated from the pressures of the majority. The most divisive moral questions should therefore be settled by the courts, not by Parliament.”

Critically evaluate and comment on this statement by reference to the theories of Rawls, Hart and Devlin covered in this course, the material on the Singapore Constitution, and any relevant illustrations.

Pick 3

Why these three

What the question is testing. The hidden tension: judges must give reasons every citizen can accept, and the statement then hands them the questions where such reasons may not exist. Public reason may still end in a vote.

Sentence 1, "the exemplar of public reason": 6.1. Rawls binds judges more strictly, but public reason also binds officials, candidates and voting citizens. So it does not follow that courts alone should decide.

Sentence 2, "insulated from the pressures of the majority": 7.3. Devlin's standard is the jury box, twelve ordinary people after discussion. Practical morality does not belong to an insulated elite, and he does not count heads either.

Sentence 3, "settled by the courts, not by Parliament": 11.5. Accept that contested rights are for Parliament, but only until the Radbruch threshold. Courts guard the floor, and rubber-stamping remains a vice.

The close ones. 11.6 gives Art 156 and binary decisions, 12.3 the legislator's duty, and 8.5 TSH's gauge.

Trap to avoid. Hart is named, but his critical morality does not say who should apply the test.

Verdict line (from the answer guide)

Insulation makes courts the right forum for clear constitutional rights and for intolerable injustice, not for the most divisive questions, which are divisive precisely because no reasons all can accept settle them; there Parliament decides, and courts guard only the floor.
