Pick 3

Why these three

What the question is testing. Two asks: do you agree, and would the theorists agree? The statement runs freestanding and value-free together, then treats the override as outside pressure.

"Freestanding", "has no authority": 6.11. This block is the answer: freestanding means derived from no single doctrine, not without moral force. Political values normally override because they are very great values.

"Convictions that citizens hold most deeply": 6.3. The override comes from within. Each reasonable doctrine affirms the conception on its own grounds, so it is no modus vivendi.

Where the statement bites: 6.2. Outside the overlap, the word "unreasonable" does the overriding. It means refusing fair terms, not being false, so a doctrine may be true and still be excluded. That is where TSH's demand for a standard has force.

The close ones. 6.1 limits the scope, 6.7 shows religion is not shut out, and 6.4 compares the theorists.

Verdict line (from the answer guide)

Disagree in the main: "freestanding" means independent of any one doctrine, not without moral authority, and for reasonable citizens the override comes from within their own convictions, through the overlapping consensus; the statement is right only at the margin, where calling a citizen "unreasonable" does the overriding and needs a moral standard Rawls does not fully supply.
