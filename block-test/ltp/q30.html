Q30 · Ruritania: mandatory death penalty for a new drug

Paper 8, Section A · This year's problem style · question 30 of 60

Ruritania is a country with a society and legal system substantially similar to Singapore’s. It imposes a mandatory death penalty for trafficking more than specified quantities of certain drugs. A new synthetic opioid, far more potent than heroin, has caused a sharp rise in overdose deaths among young people. A Ruritanian Member of Parliament proposes a bill extending the mandatory death penalty to anyone who traffics more than 5 grams of the new drug. She argues that, first, the state’s first duty is to protect life; second, a mandatory penalty treats like cases alike and removes the risk of inconsistent sentencing; and third, studies from neighbouring states show that harsh penalties reduce trafficking.

If you were also a Ruritanian Member of Parliament, how would you vote on this bill, and why? Further, if the bill were passed, would you, as a Ruritanian judge, impose the mandatory sentence on a 19-year-old courier found carrying 5.2 grams who did not know the exact quantity he carried? Explain and justify your views with reference to the relevant theories and material covered in this course.

Pick 3

Why these three

What the question is testing. Two hats. The MP must answer three grounds for a mandatory death penalty; the judge faces a 19-year-old who did not know the quantity. The catch is that ground 1 cuts against the bill.

MP, "the state's first duty is to protect life": 5.3. Life is a basic good, and for Finnis a death sentence chooses directly against it, so the bill's own premise cuts against it. Qualify with Liu, who finds the line artificial.

MP, "treats like cases alike": 3.5. Mandatory sentences buy certainty, not justice. Is a courier at 5.2 grams truly like a kingpin? The line is just only if it tracks real differences in harm or fault.

Judge, "impose the mandatory sentence": 12.4. Climb the ladder. Validity first, then interpretation: does the Act require knowledge? Refusal comes only at the Radbruch threshold, which no broad consensus reaches. Apply it if you must, and say candidly that it is harsh.

The close ones. 7.8 gives Hart's two questions, 5.4 answers the deterrence studies, and 11.2 gives the constitutional route.

Trap to avoid. Drug supply pulls you to 7.2, which is about paternalism, not severity.

Verdict line (from the answer guide)

As an MP I would vote against the mandatory penalty, because a fixed death sentence at an arbitrary weight treats unlike offenders alike and, for Finnis, chooses against the good it claims to protect; as a judge I would apply it if no knowledge requirement can be read in, saying candidly that the result is harsh, since it does not reach the threshold that alone licenses refusal.
