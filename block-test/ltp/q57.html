Q57 · Roles exist so that people can do what would otherwise be wrong

Paper 15, Section A · This year's statement style · question 57 of 60

“Professional roles exist precisely so that people can do what would otherwise be wrong. The defence lawyer may humiliate an honest witness, the judge may sentence a man he pities, and the legislator may vote for a law he believes unjust, because each acts not as a private person but as an office-holder. To demand that office-holders act on their own consciences is to abolish the division of labour on which a legal system depends.”

Critically evaluate and comment on this statement by reference to the theories of Pepper, Thunder, Hart and Fuller covered in this course, and any relevant illustrations.

Why these three

What the question is testing. Pepper, Thunder, Hart and Fuller. The hidden tension: sentence 1 concedes the acts would otherwise be wrong, so the role needs a moral justification. If the role is justified the acts are not wrong; if it is not, the role is no excuse.

The lawyer and roles generally, "not as a private person": 10.4. To simply point to the role is to give up explaining it. Thunder accepts role morality, but the lawyer stays accountable as a human being, and a role is no pocket universe.

The legislator, "vote for a law he believes unjust": 12.3. Deciding what the law should be is the legislator's role. He judges, as a trustee, so voting against conscience has no role reason at all.

The judge and office-holders, "an office-holder": 12.5. Fidelity to law is not deference to rank. Hart's validity point and Alexy's risk effect mean officials keep their moral responsibility, and pity in sentencing is ordinary role morality.

The close ones. 10.6 limits the defence lawyer, 10.10 gives Pepper's justification, and 6.8 covers the whip.

Verdict line (from the answer guide)

Roles do license what private persons may not do, but only because, and so far as, the role serves a defensible purpose; conscience does not abolish the division of labour, it keeps each role to its point, and a legislator's role is the one place where voting against conscience has no role reason at all.
