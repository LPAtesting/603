Pick 3

Why these three

What the question is testing. A one-line Chng statement taken from Pepper, applied to two actors, judges and practising lawyers. The catch: realism has a weak and a strong sense, and it means different things for each actor.

What realism claims, and judges: 9.1. Canons and ratios leave hard cases open. But practitioners may predict and judges cannot, because they must purport to justify. So the statement holds at most for counsel.

Practising lawyers: 10.2. Advising on enforcement risk is accurate and useful. The trouble is what realism does to the limit on which Pepper's role depends: it empties the word law.

"We are all realists now": 10.8. Pepper reports the phrase as something often heard from law professors. It describes a trained outlook and does not justify it.

The close ones. 9.6 sorts rule and fact scepticism, 9.9 explains convergence, and 10.10 gives Thunder's diagnosis.

Trap to avoid. Do not give only the judges: the question names practising lawyers too.

Verdict line (from the answer guide)

We are all realists in the weak sense that rules leave leeways and facts steer outcomes, but not in the strong sense: judges must justify rather than predict, and a lawyer who treats law as mere prediction loses the limit on which even Pepper's amoral role depends.
