Pick 3

Why these three

What the question is testing. A one-line Chng question. The statement is true of the theory and false of positivists. LP identifies law; it does not say what law should be, and that gap is the whole answer.

The kernel, "legal positivism": 2.1. LP's thesis is only that there is no necessary connection, and that allows contingent links, incorporation and critique. So LP is not silent, and preferring it is itself a value choice.

"To a legislator": 12.3. TSH says non-NLT legislators may not regard themselves as bound by higher standards. Liu's reply decides it: need not is not will not. LP uses morality in one dimension, good law; NLT in two.

The illustration: 7.1. Hart told legislators what the criminal law may prohibit, but that is not positivism. His thought is modular, so a positivist could back Devlin. Positivists speak to legislators; LP itself does not.

The close ones. 8.7 shows validity does not prove rightness, 8.4 sets out the contrast, and 11.8 shows LP must borrow a moral theory.

Verdict line (from the answer guide)

True of the theory, not of positivists: as an account of what law is, LP tells a legislator only how to make valid law, not what to enact; but by separating validity from rightness it keeps the moral question open rather than closing it, and positivists such as Hart have said a great deal to legislators, drawing on a moral theory that LP neither supplies nor forbids.
