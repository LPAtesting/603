Pick 3

Why these three

What the question is testing. A one-line Chng statement. Agree in part: no legal form can replace good judges and officials. But legality also disciplines bad ones, and that is the half the statement misses.

The kernel, "the people who administer it": 9.6. Fact scepticism says the fact-finder decides whether a rule applies, and appeal only moves discretion up a level. That indicts judges who fail to justify, not law itself.

"Only as good as": 3.9. Legal form binds rulers too. Published rules give notice and invite criticism, and Liu says law drags the monster into the light.

Officials and administrators: 12.5. Fidelity to law is not deference to rank. A bad law can be resisted by good officials, and pointing to the role is no excuse.

The close ones. 3.7 gives Fuller's many hands, 9.1 shows law constraining judges, and 10.4 gives Thunder's accountable lawyer.

Verdict line (from the answer guide)

Partly agree: no legal form can replace good judges, officials and lawyers, but legality also disciplines bad ones, by publishing what they do; law is better than its administrators when its rules are good, and worse when they are not.
