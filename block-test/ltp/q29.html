Q29 · Tax minimisation is admirable; lawfulness is the only question

Paper 8, Section A · This year's statement style · question 29 of 60

“Advising a client how to arrange its affairs so as to pay as little tax as the law allows is not merely permissible but admirable: it vindicates both the client’s autonomy and the rule of law. A lawyer who adds moral commentary to such advice wastes the client’s money and patronises him. The only ethical question a transactional lawyer need ask is whether the scheme is lawful.”

Critically evaluate and comment on this statement by reference to the theories of Pepper and Thunder covered in this course, any other relevant theories, and any relevant illustrations.

Pick 3

Why these three

What the question is testing. The hidden tension: sentence 1 calls tax planning admirable, a moral evaluation, while sentence 3 says lawfulness is the only ethical question. If lawfulness were the only question, admirable would be unearned.

Sentence 1, "not merely permissible but admirable": 10.1. Pepper defends access to law as justified, not every use of it as admirable. And spotting his extreme cases means weighing morality all the time, so the role is less amoral than it looks.

Sentence 2, "moral commentary": 10.3. Pepper himself recommends dialogue. The lawyer is a moral educator who neither judges nor polices, and the corporate officer is the client most open to it. It costs money, which is the kernel of the statement.

Sentence 3, "the only ethical question": 10.5. Lawfulness is the floor. Conduct rules presuppose a purpose, and for Thunder norms exploited against justice are subverted.

The close ones. 10.9 explains why no one checks the client, 10.10 contrasts mindsets, and 10.2 undermines lawful.

Trap to avoid. The floor line pulls you to 11.7, but that block is about the Constitution.

Verdict line (from the answer guide)

Lawful tax planning is a legitimate service, but it is not admirable merely because lawful: lawfulness is the floor, and above it a frank word about a scheme that exploits the law against its purpose is part of the role, not an impertinence.
