Q23 · Four walls and no rights out of whole cloth

Paper 6, Section B · Past year, TSH style · question 23 of 60

Pick 3

Why these three

What the question is testing. Do you agree with the courts' approach, and what do the theories suggest? You must cite local cases and provisions. The catch: reading the text alone cannot settle what "law" means.

"Foreign values", "within its own four walls": 11.4. Colin Chan read the Constitution that way, but TSH's reading of Haw Tua Tau shows that need not exclude universal standards, if confined to manifest injustice.

"Rights out of whole cloth": 11.2. YVK (2015) is the authority. Its target was US substantive due process, and reading Art 9 as whatever is passed begs the question. Stopping at the text is a choice of theory.

"Anything more is judicial legislation": 11.5. Accept that contested rights are for Parliament, but only until the Radbruch threshold. Rubber-stamping is a vice too, and intolerable injustice must fall.

The close ones. 11.1 gives Art 4 and the TRAP, 11.3 gives LMS on Art 12, and 12.2 names the twin vices.

Verdict line (from the answer guide)

I agree that courts should not create rights out of whole cloth, but "within its own four walls" cannot settle what "law", "life" or "equal" mean; reading those terms is a choice of theory, and the narrowest defensible floor is TSH's: presume validity, but treat manifest injustice by broad historical consensus as outside "law".
