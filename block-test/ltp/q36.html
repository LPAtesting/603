Q36 · Singapore's legislators are pragmatists, not philosophers

Paper 9, Section B · Past year, TSH style · question 36 of 60

“Singapore’s handling of section 377A, from its retention in 2007 to its repeal in 2022, shows that its legislators are pragmatists, not philosophers. In a multiracial and multireligious society, pragmatism rather than principle is the right way to deal with moral controversy.”

To what extent is this an accurate account of the 2007 and 2022 debates? Is pragmatism a defensible approach to legislating on moral questions? Why these three

What the question is testing. How accurate is "pragmatists, not philosophers", and is pragmatism defensible? You must cite the debates, local cases and provisions. The catch: pragmatism describes the method, and the reasons were moral.

2007, "its retention in 2007": 7.6. The government kept s 377A and did not proactively enforce it. But the debate had moved to rights, and its motive was a point of equivocation, so the label does not capture the reasons.

2022, "its repeal in 2022": 11.6. Repeal came with Art 156, which shielded marriage laws. That is a principled shape: punishment withdrawn on a Wolfenden-like ground, and marriage promoted. Art 5(2) shows the question moved rather than disappeared.

"Pragmatism rather than principle": 2.7. Pragmatism is itself a theory. Treating law as whatever a court says glosses over guiding principles. Concede the place of prudence: Finnis allows restraint, and Devlin wants slowness.

The close ones. 7.7 gives the costs, 7.9 the gradient behind 2022, and 12.3 the legislator's duty.

Verdict line (from the answer guide)

Pragmatism describes the method, not the reasons: the 2022 package has a principled shape, punishment withdrawn on a Wolfenden-like ground and marriage promoted, and calling it pragmatic hides the moral judgments that made it defensible.
