Q4 · Officer of the court, zealous within the law

Paper 1, Section B · Past year, TSH style · question 4 of 60

“A lawyer is an officer of the court and a servant of justice, not a hired gun. But he serves justice best by serving his client zealously within the law, and by leaving the justice of the outcome to the judge.”

Is this view of the lawyer’s role coherent? Does it make any difference to how lawyers should conduct litigation, and to how they should advise clients in non-contentious work? Critically comment by reference to relevant theories and material covered in this course.

Pick 3

Why these three

What the question is testing. Three asks: is the view coherent, does it change litigation, and does it change advice? The statement joins Thunder's first sentence to Pepper's second. The catch is that most legal work never reaches a judge.

Coherence, "leaving the justice of the outcome to the judge": 10.11. Liu's three questions test when that move works. Moving an unjust result to the institution only shifts the question, and the lawyer's part is good only if truth or dignity justifies the institution.

"How lawyers should conduct litigation": 10.6. In court the statement holds. Defending the accused is the role working, and Pepper and Thunder agree on that result. Refusal belongs only to an unjust system or an intolerably unjust rule.

"Non-contentious work": 10.9. Here it fails. A half-truth that is safe in court may be misrepresentation in a sale, and realism is likelier where no judge or opposing counsel checks the client.

The close ones. 10.2 illustrates, 10.3 supplies the remedy, and 10.10 explains the first sentence.

Trap to avoid. "The judge" pulls you to 12.4, but the actor is the lawyer.

Verdict line (from the answer guide)

The view is coherent for litigation in a basically just system, where the judge and the opposing side check the client; in advisory work no one else checks, so the lawyer cannot leave justice to anyone and must add dialogue to advice.
