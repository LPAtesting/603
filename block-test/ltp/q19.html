Q19 · Hart's minimum content v Finnis's basic goods

Paper 5, Section B · Past year, TSH style · question 19 of 60

“Hart’s ‘minimum content of natural law’ and Finnis’s basic goods both start from facts about human nature. Hart draws from those facts only the few rules that any society needs in order to survive. Finnis claims to draw a whole morality from them, and so breaks Hume’s rule that no ‘ought’ can be derived from an ‘is’.”

Is this a fair account of Hart and Finnis? Can facts about human nature tell legislators what the law should be? Critically comment by reference to relevant theories and material covered in the course.

Pick 3

Why these three

What the question is testing. Is the account fair, and can facts tell legislators what law should be? The planted error is the last sentence: Finnis derives nothing from nature, while Hart's minimum is the one that reasons from facts.

"Breaks Hume's rule": 5.6. Finnis infers nothing from urges; many answer to no good. Concede Harris's point that facts about nature form part of the reflection, though Harris still calls it reasoning.

"The few rules that any society needs": 4.4. Hume's gap is only logical. Hart's minimum is an agreed ought-premise, survival, plus facts, which the gap allows. That is why it is thin.

"Tell legislators what the law should be": 5.5. Facts fix the stakes; they do not set the goal. Finnis's common good excludes many arrangements, but value premises do the excluding.

The close ones. 5.1 says the goods are self-evident, 8.7 shows where the two part, and 5.2 shows why Finnis's floor is richer.

Verdict line (from the answer guide)

Unfair to Finnis and too kind to Hart: Finnis grasps his goods by practical reason rather than inferring them from facts, though facts about human nature shape the reflection; Hart's minimum does draw an ought from facts, but only by assuming survival as the aim, which is why it is thin enough to coexist with slavery. Facts about human nature inform legislators, but never settle what the law should be without a value premise.
