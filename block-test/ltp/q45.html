Q45 · Lives can't be planned; satisfy preferences; let him take the pill

Paper 12, Section A · This year's statement style · question 45 of 60

“Good lives cannot be planned: people flourish by following what they want from moment to moment, and a state that asks them to think about their lives as a whole is being paternalistic. The law should therefore aim simply at satisfying citizens’ preferences, whatever they are. If a citizen would be happiest taking a legal pill that keeps him permanently content but uninterested in work, friends or learning, the law should let him.”

Critically evaluate and comment on this statement by reference to the theories of Finnis, Hart and Rawls covered in this course, and any relevant illustrations.

Pick 3

Why these three

What the question is testing. The hidden tension: sentence 1 denies lives can be planned, yet sentence 3 justifies the pill by what would make the citizen happiest, a whole-life judgment.

Sentence 1, "from moment to moment": 5.6. Finnis infers nothing from urges, and it is unreasonable to live merely by immediate cravings. Liu adds that the plan sets policies, not a deathbed test for each lunch.

Sentence 2, "whatever they are": 5.4. Maximising preferences is reasonable only inside the other requirements, as efficiency. The pill is the experience machine: a sensible person wants to do certain things, though Liu warns that intuition is a fragile proof.

Sentence 3, "a legal pill": 7.2. Hart gives the legal answer: paternalism protects defective consent. A pill that ends the capacity for future choice undermines consent, so restrict supply rather than prohibit use.

The close ones. 5.1 names what the pill gives up, 5.5 rebuts the paternalism charge, and 6.1 brings in Rawls.

Verdict line (from the answer guide)

The statement smuggles in the planning it denies: "happiest" is a whole-life verdict. Finnis shows why a contented, idle life loses real goods, and Hart why consent to the pill is suspect; the defensible conclusion is regulation of supply, not indifference and not prohibition.
