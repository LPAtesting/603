Q51 · Law is a purposive enterprise

Paper 13, Section B · Past year, TSH style · question 51 of 60

“Law is a purposive enterprise. Those who make, apply and argue about the law cannot perform their roles well without a view about what law is for.”

To what extent is this statement true? What would it require of legislators, judges and lawyers respectively? Is a purposive view of law consistent with the separation of law and morals? Critically comment by reference to relevant theories and material covered in the course.

Pick 3

Why these three

What the question is testing. Three asks: is the statement true, what does it require of each actor, and is it consistent with separating law and morals? The catch: the last answer depends on whether the purpose is order or justice.

"Cannot perform their roles well": 2.6. Fish says marshalling rules takes place within a purposive enterprise, and Leff that you cannot judge excellence without knowing what is being done. Theory is used even when it is not cited.

"The separation of law and morals": 3.3. If the purpose is function, a positivist can accept it: critics say Fuller's concern is really efficacy. If the purpose is justice, as when inner and outer morality decay together, purpose becomes a ground of law.

Lawyers, and the purpose of the role: 10.10. Thunder's law is intended for ends naturally good for man. Pepper's access and autonomy are a purpose too, so the two differ in mindset rather than in whether law has a point.

The close ones. 8.2 states the separation side, 9.8 covers judges, and 5.5 gives the legislator's common good.

Verdict line (from the answer guide)

True: no one can make, apply or argue law well without a view of what it is for; whether that view breaches the separation thesis depends on whether the purpose is order, which a positivist can accept, or justice, which makes purpose a ground of law.
