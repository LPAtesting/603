- Only if the collector proves she came to Singapore for business || Purpose matters only if the claimant lured her here. A voluntary visit for pleasure is enough.
* Yes, as of right: she was present when served, whatever the length of the visit || Correct: s 16(1)(a)(i) with O 7 r 2(1)(a). The weak connection goes to a stay, not to existence.

Q: Version 1: the collector uses a front company to arrange a fake "investor meeting" in Singapore, then serves Priya in the hotel lobby. Version 2: Priya comes on holiday, and the collector, seeing it on Instagram, serves her at Sentosa. What is the position?

- Service is good in both, as presence at the time of service is all that matters || Presence induced by fraud is an abuse of process, so service in Version 1 may be set aside.
* Only Version 1 may be set aside; in Version 2 any unfairness goes to a stay || Correct: luring by a sham meeting is abuse; taking advantage of a voluntary visit is not.
- Both may be set aside, because the collector deliberately targeted Priya in each case || Watching for a voluntary visit is not inducement. Only Version 1 involves luring.
- Version 2 is the worse case, because the collector exploited Priya's private holiday || Exploiting a voluntary visit is legitimate. The abuse lies in inducing presence by a trick.

- The set-aside succeeds if filed within the time limits for contesting jurisdiction || Time limits protect a notice of intention to contest. They do not undo a submission already made.
