C1.6.1Evidence: characterising evidential matters

C1.7Forum mandatory rules: starter

C1.7Express scope first

C1.7Silent statute: construe it
Parliamentary Intention: Per JIO Minerals, [the statute] overrides [foreign law] if Parliament clearly intended so, as it may legislate extraterritorially, COL rules being "useful tools" to ascertain [its geographical scope].
Two Aspects of the Presumption: Per JIO Minerals, the presumption against extraterritoriality has two overlapping aspects, both yielding to clear intention that [the statute] reach [the foreign-law issue]:
Choice of law aspect: [the statute] applies only if Singapore law governs [the issue], so as [UAE law (contract)] or [Indonesian law (tort)] governed in JIO, the Misrepresentation Act did not apply.
Territorial aspect: [the statute] reaches only [conduct] within Singapore, unless displaced by clear, possibly implied, intention (Parno), as for [the TCM Practitioners Act] (Huang Danmin).
Gauging Intention: Per JIO Minerals, [a conduct-regulating / protective statute, e.g., s 37 PCA / the TCM Practitioners Act] more readily applies extraterritorially, unlike [one merely supplementing common law remedies, e.g., the MA].
C1.7Types of mandatory rule
[the Singapore statute] relied on by [party] is either:
Overriding: applies irrespective of the proper law, e.g., [export / import controls] and [s 5(2) CLA on foreign gambling contracts].
Conditional: per s 27(2) UCTA, UCTA applies despite [the foreign choice] if (a) [the term] was imposed wholly or mainly to evade it, or (b) [a party] dealt as consumer while habitually resident here and the essential steps for contracting were taken here, [by him or others on his behalf].
Contrast bona fide (C2.2.2p5): "wholly or mainly" is wider than "sole purpose", and [the chosen law] survives subject to the Act, whereas [a non-bona fide choice] is struck out.
Flip side: per s 27(1) UCTA, if [Singapore law] governs only by [the parties' choice], ss 2–7 do not apply.
