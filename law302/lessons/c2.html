Contract choice of law: the proper law and illegality

⚡ BIG IDEA

A contract is governed by its proper law, found in three stages: an express choice (virtually conclusive if bona fide, legal and not against public policy), else an implied choice, else the law of closest and most real connection. Formation, form and capacity have their own rules. Illegality can enter by seven routes: the proper law, forum public policy, Foster v Driscoll, Lemenda, Ralli Brothers, tainting, and reliance as a defence.

C2.1 STARTER: THE GOVERNING FRAMEWORK
C2.1.1 Global Starter

Per Amin Rasheed, the proper law governs [the validity / construction / effect / discharge] of [the contract] between [Party A] and [Party B]. Per Pacific Recreation (approving OUI v Turegum), it is found by (1) express choice, (2) implied choice, or (3) the closest and most real connection (Bonython).

  • Characterisation Per Re Bonacina, [an agreement unsupported by consideration] is still contractual for [conflicts purposes], the lex fori characterising in a "broad internationalist spirit" (Raiffeisen; C1.1).
  • Scope Per C&Y, the proper law governs [interpretation / incorporation of terms / performance / discharge], material validity ([duress / misrepresentation]: CIMB) and illegality under it (C2.10.1), and per s 3(1) FLPA its [limitation period] applies (C1.5).
    • Not covered: [formation] (C2.6), [form] (C2.7), [capacity] (C2.8) and procedure, e.g., [burden of proof / arguably quantum] (C1.2 to C1.4), unlike Art 9 Hague Principles, which extends to [burden of proof / pre-contractual obligations].
  • No Renvoi Per Amin Rasheed and Ong Ghee Soon, [law X] means its domestic rules, not conflicts rules remitting to [law Y] (cf Art 8 Hague Principles).
C2.1.2 Preliminary Rules

(deploy only if raised)

  • Subsequent Conduct Per Las Vegas Hilton, the proper law is fixed at contracting, so [later payments / correspondence invoking law X] is irrelevant, though per James Miller it may found an estoppel against [party].
  • Non-National Law Per Musawi and C&Y, only a country's law (including [a state within a federation]) qualifies, so a choice of [Sharia law / lex mercatoria] fails as the proper law, leaving [IPL / OPL].
    • Contrast: [arbitrators] may apply chosen "rules of law" (Art 28(1) Model Law), and Art 3 Hague Principles allows [UNIDROIT-type rules] unless forum law bars them, as [Singapore's common law] does.
    • Fallback: incorporate [the rules] as terms of a contract governed by [law Y] (C2.2.9).
C2.2 STAGE 1: EXPRESS CHOICE OF LAW
C2.2.1 Starter

Per Peh Teck Quee, applying Vita Food, cl [X]'s choice of [law X] is virtually conclusive unless not (a) bona fide, (b) legal, or (c) consistent with public policy, each tested by the lex fori on objective intention, so [D]'s claim not to have [seen the standard terms] is irrelevant.

  • Unrelated Law Permitted Per Peh Teck Quee, [law X] needs no connection (cf Art 2(4) Hague Principles), as [the parties] may want [a neutral law / a developed law on the subject] (Amin Rasheed).
  • Limbs (b) and (c) Per C&Y, "legal" is of unclear meaning and the public policy limb duplicates [the general reservation] (C2.10.2), so [the attacking party] leads with [bona fides].
C2.2.2 Attack: Not Bona Fide

(if it succeeds, go to the OPL (C2.2.8))

  • The Argument Per Peh Teck Quee, choosing [law X] to evade [the moneylending / exchange control / consumer laws of country Y] defeats the clause only if evasion was the sole purpose.
    • "Another law": per C&Y, usually [the OPL], but possibly [a forum mandatory rule] (Golden Acres), whose evasion fails twice: [the rule] applies anyway and [the clause] is struck out.
  • Scenario A: Bona Fide (+) As in Peh Teck Quee (bank operating, facility booked, accounts kept and tax paid in Singapore), [place of booking / performance / incorporation / headquarters] genuinely connects [law X], so the clause stands.
  • Scenario B: Not Bona Fide (−) [No / negligible] connection and [the timing of insertion / the sole beneficiary of evasion] show that evading [the OPL] was the sole purpose, so the clause is struck out completely, unlike under s 27(2) UCTA (C2.2.7).
C2.2.3 Attack: Vitiating Factor Infecting the Clause

(material validity: the proper law governs, so ask whether the clause survives)

  • Separability Rule Per Maldives Airports (cf Art 7 Hague Principles), cl [X] belongs to the separable dispute resolution framework, so per CIMB [misrepresentation / duress / mistake] vitiating [the contract] does not necessarily infect it.
  • The Test Per C&Y, the crux is whether [the vitiating factor] leaves no consensus on the clause itself, not whether [the contract] is void or voidable; if infected, [the OPL] governs material validity.
  • Disputed vs Agreed Per CIMB, a merely disputed [fraud / misrepresentation] leaves [law X] applying "as if the contract were valid", lest a bare allegation neutralise the clause, unlike an agreed absence of [any agreement].
  • Scenario A: Clause Survives (+) Per CIMB and C&Y, [fraud as to the subject matter / a common fundamental mistake about the goods] affects [the contract] but not consensus on [law X], so it governs.
  • Scenario B: Clause Falls (−) Per CIMB, [non est factum] infects everything, and per C&Y [no matching offer and acceptance] arguably does too (left open in Maldives Airports), so go to [the OPL].
  • Overlap: Duress Per Royal Boskalis, [extreme duress, e.g., a human-shield threat] engages forum public policy (C2.10.2), whereas per Dimskal [economic duress] stays with [the proper law].
C2.2.4 Attack: Unclear Expression of Choice
  • Lex Fori Interprets Per Enka v Chubb and Compagnie Tunisienne, the lex fori's ordinary interpretation rules construe [ambiguous cl X], the proper law being a connecting factor, as "[law of the flag]" with [several flags] meant the flag of the vessels primarily used.
C2.2.5 Attack: Floating Proper Law
  • Floating Clause Void As contracts cannot exist "in a legal vacuum" (Amin Rasheed), cl [X] applying [law A or law B] on [one party's election / a future event] is "meaningless" and of no legal effect (Shanghai Turbo), so skip Stage 2 (C2.2.8).
    • No election mechanism: as in Shanghai Turbo (cl 17: "laws of Singapore/or People's Republic of China"), [the missing mechanism] adds to [the uncertainty].
  • Which Law Tests Validity? (argue both) Per C&Y, the Shanghai Turbo HC on balance used the lex fori (also testing [both candidate laws]), whereas The Iran Vojdan used [the OPL] (German law: void as small print, though it permits floating clauses; Iranian, presumed English: void as floating).
    • Lex fori (−): per Prof Chong, under Singapore law [cl X] is invalid even with [an election mechanism], so [the OPL] governs.
    • OPL (+): per Prof Chong, if [the OPL, e.g., law Z] permits floating clauses, [cl X] is valid and [law A] governs once [party] [elects / sues first].
  • Paired Jurisdiction Clause Per The Iran Vojdan, [jurisdiction options] "intimately connected" with the law choice fall with it, as the Shanghai Turbo HC held of [the "and … /or" pairing] (contrast [separate sub-clauses] in The Frank Pais).
    • Rebuttal: the Shanghai Turbo CA severed [the jurisdiction part] as not interdependent, backed by [the severance clause], reading it as submission to whichever named court is seised, so [the resisting party] must show strong cause (J4).
  • Contract Survives Per Shanghai Turbo and The Iran Vojdan, striking out [cl X] leaves [the contract] under [the OPL], though per C&Y, if [no law at all] was intended, "a contract without a law is no contract at all".
  • Distinguish a Change of Proper Law (C2.2.6) In theory [a floating clause] has no law until crystallisation (void), while [a change] varies an existing law (valid), but per C&Y the line is fine, as [the OPL] operates meanwhile in both.
C2.2.6 Variation: Change of Proper Law / Split Proper Law
  • Change Per Kredietbank v Sinotani (and James Miller), [the parties] may change [law A] to [law B], though whether [the lex fori] or [the original proper law] decides if the change is allowed is open.
    • Limits: per the SAL Report, Singapore should adopt Art 2(3) Hague Principles: no prejudice to [formal validity] or [third-party rights], which suggests a lex fori conflicts question.
  • Split / Dépeçage Per Libyan Arab Foreign Bank, [law A] may govern [one part] and [law B] [the rest] (cf Art 2(2) Hague Principles), but per Kahler a split is not inferred "readily or without good reason", parties expecting one law (Enka).
    • Coherence: the split must be internally coherent, so not [different laws on repudiation] depending on [which party is innocent].
C2.2.7 Overlay: Forum Mandatory Rule Superimposed

(full analysis in C1.7)

  • Superimposed, Not Struck Out Per s 27(2) UCTA, the Act applies despite [the law X clause] if [the clause] was imposed wholly or mainly to evade it, or [a consumer] habitually resident here dealt and the essential steps were taken here, [by him or on his behalf], [law X] then applying subject to UCTA.
    • Flip side: per s 27(1) UCTA, if [Singapore law] governs only by [the parties' choice], ss 2 to 7 do not apply.
C2.2.8 Effect of an Ineffective Express Choice: Straight to the OPL
  • Skip Stage 2 Per Shanghai Turbo and The Iran Vojdan, as [the parties] turned their minds to [the governing law] and failed ([not bona fide / floating / infected]), inferring a choice is "artificial", so go to Stage 3.
    • Low stakes: per Pacific Recreation, skipping Stage 2 is "not as significant a step as it seems", as [the same factors] recur at [Stage 3].
C2.2.9 Distinguish: Choice vs Incorporation of Foreign Law
  • The Distinction Per C&Y, a choice of [law X] applies it as living law ([later amendments apply]), whereas incorporating [UCP 600 / Sharia principles] makes them terms that crystallise at formation unless [agreed otherwise].
  • Validity and Interpretation Per Beximco, [non-state rules] may be incorporated if [the proper law] permits and they are sufficiently certain, being construed by [the proper law] with regard to [their home system].
  • Application Cl [X] [chooses a law / incorporates rules into a contract governed by law Y], so [the non-national rules work as terms (C2.1.2) / the pre-amendment version applies].
C2.3 STAGE 2: IMPLIED CHOICE OF LAW
C2.3.1 Starter

Per Pacific Recreation, absent cl [X], a choice of [law Y] may be inferred from [the terms] and [the circumstances], weighing Las Vegas Hilton factors of differing weight. Per Enka v Chubb, an implied choice is as effective as an express one, and the Vita Food limits apply (C2.2.1).

C2.3.2 Applying the Factors

(weightiest first)

  • Jurisdiction / Arbitration Clause (heavy) Per Pacific Recreation, submitting to [the courts of / arbitration in] [country Y] implies [law Y], the chosen forum being presumed to apply its own law.
    • Contrast: per Art 4 Hague Principles, [such a clause] is "not in itself equivalent to a choice of law", so [the opposing party] urges [Stage 3].
  • Standard Form / Terms of Art (strong) Per Lord Diplock in Amin Rasheed, [a Lloyd's SG form scheduled to the MIA 1906 / terms of art peculiar to law X] imply [law X].
    • Caveat: per Lord Wilberforce, [a universally used form] shows nothing, reaching [English law] only at Stage 3, so argue the stage per [client].
  • Related Contract (can be decisive) Per JIO Minerals, [Contract A] so closely related to [Contract B under law X] ([sent to the buyer / cited three times / essential]) takes [law X], even between different parties.
  • Language and Currency (little) Per Pacific Recreation, [English] (the lingua franca) and [USD] carry little weight, unlike [an esoteric language or currency], and per Lupofresh universal terms like ["cif UK port"] are neutral.
  • Residence, Business, Purpose Per Las Vegas Hilton, [the parties' residence / place of business] and [the commercial purpose] count, but per Pacific Recreation [the place of contracting] matters little unless [performance] is also there.
  • Form Pointing Away Per Pacific Recreation, [a deed / an indemnity] unknown to [law X] points away from it: "why would parties intentionally use forms and terms inapposite to the governing law?"
C2.3.3 Limits on Inference
  • Pointing Away Is Not Pointing Towards Per Pacific Recreation, factors negating [Chinese law] did not imply [Singapore law], [deeds being known to all common law systems], so the court moved to Stage 3.
  • Principle of Validation Per C&Y (cf Pacific Recreation), parties presumed to intend a valid contract (ut res magis valeat) chose [law X], under which [the contract] and [each term] are valid.
    • Limits: per C&Y, not where [formation] or [a vitiating factor] is disputed, and arguably irrelevant at Stage 3 (Coast Lines v Hudig, Megaw LJ; contra [Lord Denning]), which seeks [connection, not intention].
C2.4 STAGE 3: OBJECTIVE PROPER LAW
C2.4.1 Starter

Per Bonython, [the contract] is governed by the law with which [the transaction] has its closest and most real connection, being per Pacific Recreation (approving The Assunzione) what just and reasonable persons would have intended had they thought about [the governing law], weighing [the same factors] as at Stage 2.

C2.4.2 Rebutting Objections at Stage 3
  • Weak Stage 2 Factor Now Decisive Per Pacific Recreation, Stage 3 imputes rather than finds a choice, so [the deed being unknown to Chinese law], too weak at Stage 2, may be decisive for [law X].
    • Spectrum: per Amin Rasheed (Lord Wilberforce) and Lew, Stages 2 and 3 "merge", so [party] may argue [law X] at either.
  • Vita Food Limits Inapplicable Per C&Y, they police [a choice], so [the counterparty] cannot attack [the OPL] as not bona fide.
  • Not Confined to the Parties' Candidates Per Pacific Recreation and JIO Minerals, the court may apply [a third law, e.g., US / UAE law], but only after inviting [the parties'] submissions (natural justice).
  • Wrong Factors Per Prof Chong, [witness convenience / patent registration] are Spiliada factors (J4), irrelevant to [the OPL] unless connected to [the contract].
  • No Ties Per Lew, "courts do not in practice declare ties", so even [a slight balance] picks [law X].
C2.4.3 Weighting
  • Critique of "Equal Weight" Per C&Y, Pacific Recreation's "equal weight" dictum is puzzling, as [the place of performance] carries great weight (Bonython; Enka), and later cases weigh [factors] differentially.
    • Illustration: in Shanghai Turbo, [performance in China] was offset by [the SGX-listed counterparty / references to Singapore statutes], the CA finding a good arguable case for [Singapore law].
  • Application Per Prof Chong, the strongest argument is usually [the place where both parties perform, e.g., country X], supported by [place of contracting / currency / incorporation and head office / related transaction], so [law X] is the OPL.
  • Balance of Factors As in Pacific Recreation, factors pointing away from [law A] far exceed those pointing away from [law B], so [law B] is the OPL.
    • Comparative (analogy only): Art 4 Rome I applies [fixed rules], else [the characteristic performer's habitual residence], subject to a manifestly closer connection, usable only by analogy for [law Y].
C2.5 PROMISSORY CHOICE OF LAW CLAUSE (only if a party relies on breach of the clause itself)
  • Default: Declaratory Per Ace Insurance, cl [X] is declaratory, as [an inferred choice] cannot contain a promise and party autonomy is fettered anyway; only very clear language makes it promissory, which "governed by the law of [country X]" is not.
  • When Breached? Per C&Y, [the promise's content] is construed under [the proper law], clearly breached by seising [a court] whose conflicts rules ignore cl [X], and arguably one superimposing its overriding mandatory rules.
  • Consequences Per C&Y, if promissory: damages, an ASI on the stronger breach basis (J6), and arguably a defence to [recognition / enforcement] of [the foreign judgment] (J7).
  • Still Separable Even if declaratory, cl [X] belongs to [the dispute resolution suite] alongside [the jurisdiction clause], so is separable (C2.2.3).
C2.6 FORMATION DISPUTES: THE PUTATIVE PROPER LAW (use if the contract's existence is disputed)
  • Putative Proper Law As [the dispute] concerns existence, per Lew v Nargolwala a law agreed during negotiations governs (rare), failing which a JIO-style three-stage test applied to [the negotiations / transaction] finds the putative proper law, matching The Parouth and CIMB.
  • Application [The alleged express clause / prior dealings on law X terms / closest connection of the negotiations] identifies [law X], under which [the contract] [was / was not] formed because [silence constitutes acceptance / the offer was accepted].
    • Double bootstraps (Prof Chong): [law X] first decides whether [the law X clause] was incorporated, then whether [the contract] was formed.
  • Counter: Lex Fori Per Lew, applying [the lex fori] regardless of [any connection to the forum] is "illogical" and defeats uniformity, so it has no role, even residually.
  • Counter: Hybrids Lew also rejected Yeo's lex fori fallback where [the entire contract] is disputed, and Briggs's lex fori test, which treats [the proper law] as purely subjective.
  • Grave-Injustice Caveat Where [law X] binds [D] by [silence], Lew left open "a very small caveat, very rarely applicable", whose effect is no contract under any law, not [the lex fori]; per C&Y, use [forum public policy] instead.
    • Cf soft law: Art 10(2) Rome I and Art 6(2) Hague Principles let [a party] rely on [its habitual residence / establishment law] to deny consent.
  • Battle of Forms: Clashing Putative Laws [A]'s [law X clause] and [B]'s [law Y clause] may each validate themselves, and "it makes no sense to decide which one to choose by any putative law" (Dornoch).
    • Lex fori: The Heidberg and Dornoch applied [the lex fori], but Lew now bars [that fallback].
    • Putative OPL: per Prof Chong, rejected in The Heidberg as no more correct than [arbitrarily preferring one clause], so [party] needs [another tiebreak].
    • Yeo (deadlock overstated): map each law's [first shot / last shot / knock-out] rule; both clauses apply only where each validates itself; where neither survives, use [the putative OPL], then [the lex fori].
    • Art 6(1)(b) Hague Principles: [the law X clause] applies only if [its standard terms] prevail under both laws, else no choice.
  • Critique: Lex Fori in a Subsidiary Role Per Prof Chong (and Teo), [the negotiations alone] give too few factors, and [non est factum / a voiding mistake] favours one side, so [the lex fori] should decide agreement on [the putative clause] and break [battle of forms] ties.
  • Consideration: Formation or Validity? Per C&Y, [consideration] goes to validity under [the proper law] (Re Bonacina), but AQCEL treated it as formation for [the putative law], which matters only if [the two laws] differ.
  • Distinguish Material Validity A contract voidable for [fraud / duress / misrepresentation] was formed, so [its proper law] governs "as if the contract were valid" (CIMB), formation and validity being distinct (contrast PT First Media).
C2.7 FORMAL VALIDITY (formal requirement, e.g., [writing / signature / witnessing / notarisation / official stamp / wet-ink vs e-signature / mode of execution of a deed])
  • What Counts as "Form" Per the Giuliano-Lagarde Report, "every external manifestation required" of one expressing the will to be bound, so [the requirement] is [form / substance].
  • Alternative Reference Rule Per AQCEL (adopting PT Jaya Putra's obiter), meeting [requirement X] under either [the proper law] or [the lex loci contractus] suffices, parties rarely thinking about [formalities].
    • Not form: per PT Jaya Putra, [a government approval requirement] goes to validity under [the proper law].
  • Trap: Form vs Capacity Per AQCEL, [a missing witness under s 41B CA] has "no relevance" to the will to be bound, so it is form; per Integral Petroleum, [a joint prokurist's missing signature] has "real substance", so it is capacity (C2.8.2).
  • Locating the Place of Contracting Per Transniko, the lex fori places it where [acceptance] is received, [country X], Art 11(2) Rome I's wider list ([either party's presence / residence]) not being Singapore law.
  • Trap: "No Action Shall Be Brought" Per Leroux v Brown (and Star City's right vs enforceability test), s 6 CLA (no "action" on [a guarantee / land sale] without signed writing) is procedural, binding [the foreign-law contract] here.
    • Rebuttal: per C&Y, on a functional view (Goh Suan Hee), [s 6] may be substantive form (cf the criticism in Monterosso), so [the proper law / lex loci contractus] governs (C1.2.3).
  • Distinguish [Form] has alternative validating laws, [a procedural formality] binds every Singapore action, and [a forum mandatory rule] applies because [the statute] overrides (C1.7).
  • Immovables [The contract to sell land] follows this section, but [the conveyance's formalities] follow the lex situs.
C2.8 CAPACITY
C2.8.1 Natural Persons

(use if [individual] pleads [minority / mental incapacity])

  • Options Per C&Y, the rule is unsettled among [the lex domicilii] (unfair to [an unaware counterparty]), [the lex loci contractus] (fortuitous or unidentifiable [online]) and [the proper law] (the modern trend: Charron; Bodley Head).
  • Preferred: Objective Proper Law Per C&Y, as [the stronger party] may impose a law under which [the vulnerable party] has capacity, the objective proper law should govern, under which [Party] [had / lacked] capacity.
    • Alternative rules: [domicile or place of contracting] (Guatemala v Nunez) or [domicile or OPL] (Dicey; ALRC) would validate like form, but courts have not adopted them.
  • Immovables: Bank of Africa v Cohen The lex situs governed capacity to contract about [land], but per C&Y it should govern only capacity to convey, so [the damages claim] under [the English proper law] survives.
C2.8.2 Corporations
  • Dual Test Per C&Y (Dicey r 162), [Corporation] needs capacity under both [the law of incorporation] and [the proper law], a subjective proper law being acceptable as [companies] are less exploitable.
  • Broad Meaning of Capacity Per the Haugesund Kommune majority, capacity is the internationalist "legal ability to exercise specific rights", so [a statutory bar on swaps] removed [Corporation]'s capacity for [that contract], though [Norwegian law] lacks ultra vires.
    • Rule classification: per Auld LJ in Macmillan, ask whether [s 50 / the foreign provision] is truly a capacity rule or [a mere limit on power] (C1.1.4).
  • Consequences: The Proper Law Per Haugesund, once [the law of incorporation] denies capacity, [the proper law, English law] decides the consequences, so [the contract] is void, but [the lender] recovers [the sums advanced] in unjust enrichment, [change of position] failing.
  • Counter: Etherton LJ's Dissent "Capacity" should bear its domestic ultra vires meaning, especially under [a chosen English law], avoiding the "bizarre result" of [swaps] valid under [English and Norwegian domestic law] yet void.
    • Response: per C&Y, prefer the majority, consistent with Macmillan and Raiffeisen, despite [the anomaly] on [these facts].
  • Singapore Company Per ss 23, 25 and 25B CA, [a Singapore company] has full capacity subject to [its constitution], and no act is invalid only for lack of capacity, protecting [a good-faith counterparty].

C2.10 ILLEGALITY
C2.10.1 Routing Starter

[Party resisting enforcement] pleads illegality against [the claim on the contract] via (A) the proper law, (B) forum international public policy, (C) Foster, (D) Lemenda, (E) Ralli, (F) tainting, or (G) reliance as a defence.

  • Court's Own Motion Per Fan Ren Ray (approving Edler v Auerbach), the court may refuse [an ex facie illegal contract] though unpleaded, but [extraneous evidence of an illegal object] must be pleaded.
  • Route A: Proper Law Per Kahler and Zivnostenka, illegality under [the proper law] bars enforcement, unless [that illegality rule] itself offends Singapore public policy (The Playa Larga; Re Helbert Wagg).
  • Irrelevant Laws Per Sim See Kee (and Kleinwort), illegality at [the place of contracting / a party's residence or incorporation] is irrelevant; conversely, per Dimskal, legality at [the LLS] cannot save [a contract illegal under the proper law / a forum mandatory rule / forum international policy].
  • Framework: Foster vs Ralli vs Lemenda Per C&Y, breach of foreign written law [intended at formation] goes to Foster and [in required performance] to Ralli, while foreign public policy alone goes to Lemenda; per Magdeev, "Ralli looks to action, Foster … to intention".
    • Foster: international policy and comity; wicked intention at formation; [the real intended place]; bars [the guilty party].
    • Ralli: per Esben, an independent conflicts rule on [the required act] at [the contractual LLS], intention irrelevant.
    • Lemenda: domestic policy coinciding with [the LLS's policy], at [the place of contemplated performance].
    • Wrinkle: Esben calls comity a "fundamental domestic public policy", blurring Peh Teck Quee's split between [Ralli (no public policy)] and [Foster (forum public policy)].
  • Recognition, Not Enforcement Per OUB v Chua, [the foreign penal / revenue law] is recognised as fact in [routes (A), (C) and (F)], not enforced, unless so objectionable that breaking it is "meritorious" (Lord Reid in Regazzoni).
C2.10.2 Route B: Repugnant to Forum International Public Policy
  • Extreme Duress Per Royal Boskalis, [the Iraqi-law finalisation agreement] procured by [a human-shield threat] was refused, as "any civilised tribunal" would refuse it; per Westacre, so is [a contract for terrorism / drug trafficking / corruption / fraud] under any [proper law].

1Start here: C2 in plain English

Most Question 2 issues are about a contract: Is the choice of law clause valid? Was the contract ever formed? Is it in the right form? Did a party have capacity? Is it illegal? Each question has its own law. The main one is the contract's proper law.

★ IN PLAIN ENGLISH

Think of the proper law as the contract's home country. The parties can name its home in the contract (express choice), or the home can be read from clues like a court clause or a standard form (implied choice), or, if there is no clue, the court finds where the contract "lives" most (closest connection). But some questions are decided by other laws: whether a contract was ever born (formation), whether it was properly dressed (form), and whether the parties were old enough or allowed to sign (capacity).

✎ WORKED EXAMPLE
Neutral choice or evasion?
  • Neutral and genuine. A Japanese seller and an Indonesian buyer choose English law because neither trusts the other's legal system and English commercial law is well developed. England has nothing to do with the deal, but no connection is needed (Amin Rasheed). The choice is bona fide and virtually conclusive.
  • Evasion. Two Malaysian companies, contracting and performing only in Malaysia, add "governed by Singapore law" the week after learning a Malaysian statute would void their deal, and nothing else explains the choice. If evading Malaysian law was the sole purpose, the clause is not bona fide and is struck out completely. The court goes straight to the objective proper law, which is likely Malaysian law, and the statute bites.

Bona fides is about why the law was chosen, not whether it is connected to the deal.

★ IN PLAIN ENGLISH

Why is a floating choice of law void when a floating choice of court can be valid (J1.1.3)? Because a contract needs a law from the moment it is made: the law decides whether it is valid, what it means and what each side must do, starting on day one. A clause that says "law A or law B, to be decided later" leaves the contract with no law in the meantime. Where would you look to decide whether the contract is valid on day one? A court, by contrast, is only needed once a dispute arises, so picking it later causes no such gap.

Reading the clues for an implied choice

A contract between Alpha (Singapore) and Beta (China) has no choice of law clause. It says disputes go to arbitration in Singapore, it is written in English, and the price is in US dollars.

  1. Singapore arbitration clause: heavy. The chosen forum is presumed to apply its own law (Pacific Recreation), so this points strongly to Singapore law.
  2. English language and USD: little. Both are used worldwide, so they say almost nothing (Lupofresh).
  3. Conclusion: an implied choice of Singapore law is likely.

Now remove the arbitration clause. English and USD alone cannot imply any choice, so the court moves to Stage 3 and looks for the closest connection.

⚠ TRAP
Pointing away is not pointing towards

In Pacific Recreation, factors negating Chinese law did not imply Singapore law (deeds are known to all common law systems), so the court moved to Stage 3. ★ IN PLAIN ENGLISH

Formation looks like a riddle: you cannot use the contract's law to decide whether the contract exists, because if it does not exist it has no law. The way out is to pretend for a moment that the contract exists, ask which law would govern it, and then use that law to decide whether it really does. That pretend law is the putative proper law. "Double bootstraps" is the same trick applied twice: the putative law first decides whether its own choice of law clause got into the deal, then whether the deal as a whole was made.

✎ WORKED EXAMPLE
Did Beta accept?

  2. Putative proper law: if there were a contract, it would include Alpha's terms choosing Singapore law. So Singapore law decides whether the Singapore-law clause was incorporated, and then whether "noted, will revert" was acceptance.
  3. Not the lex fori as a fallback (Lew), even though here the answer happens to be Singapore law anyway.

Change one fact: Beta replied on its standard form choosing Thai law. Now it is a battle of forms. Each law may validate its own clause, and Lew bars simply falling back on Singapore law, so map how each law treats the exchange (first shot, last shot, knock-out) per Yeo.

Prof Chong's query: does validity under the proper law trump the coincidence of forum and LLS policy? And it is open whether a Lemenda breach meets the tainting threshold.

★ IN PLAIN ENGLISH

Lemenda needs two locks to be closed before a contract is refused: the deal must offend Singapore's own sense of morality and the public policy of the place where it was to be performed. Paying someone to lean on government officials closes both locks. A foreign rule that is just a government's purchasing preference closes neither, because it is policy in the everyday sense, not public policy in law (Shaikh Faisal).
