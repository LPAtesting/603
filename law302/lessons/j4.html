Exercise: Spiliada, forum conveniens and strong cause

⚡ BIG IDEA

Once the court has jurisdiction, ask whether it should use it. If someone broke a jurisdiction clause, the party in breach must show strong cause. If no one did, run Spiliada: is another forum clearly more appropriate (stage 1), and would the claimant be denied substantial justice there (stage 2)? Who bears the burden depends on service in or out, and stay or set-aside.

J4.1 EXERCISE OF JURISDICTION: STAY UNDER SPILIADA
J4.1.1 Starter (must write)

As [defendant] was [served here / has submitted], jurisdiction exists as of right, so it may only seek a stay (O 6 r 7(5)(b) / r 12(4)(b)). Per Spiliada, [X] must be an available forum, clearly or distinctly more appropriate "for the interests of all the parties and the ends of justice".

  • Two Stages Per Spiliada, ask (1) whether [X] has the most real and substantial connection with [the dispute], then (2) whether [claimant] would be denied substantial justice in [X].
  • Which Test Spiliada applies absent agreement; a breached [foreign EJC] engages strong cause (J4.3); [an international arbitration clause] engages a mandatory stay (IAA s 6), not FNC.
  • Procedure and Power [Defendant] states the ground in its [defence / affidavit] (O 6 r 7(4); r 12(3)), heard first (O 9 r 7). The power is SCJA Sch 1 para 9, with O 9 r 16(1)(b) and O 3 r 2(2).
  • Waiver by Delay (+) Per Sun Jin and C&Y, [defendant]'s delay of [N months] is relevant but not decisive, read with [the steps taken]; the test is "the competing interests of the parties and the justice of the case as a whole".
  • Meaning Per Spiliada, forum non conveniens means "not the appropriate forum" for [the dispute], not mere practical convenience such as [the cost of flying witnesses from X].
  • Comparative, Not Abstract Per JIO and C&Y, [defendant] must show [X] clearly more appropriate; Singapore having [few links] is not enough. Factors [scattered across X, Y and Z] or finely balanced → no stay.
  • Two Foreign Fora Per C&Y, a stay may still go if [X] and [Y] are each clearly more appropriate, though the court does not say which is the natural forum.
  • Relativity Per JIO, the comparison is Singapore against [X], so that [law Z] governs does not help [claimant]; a third forum's equal claim does not make Singapore the natural forum.
  • Each Claim Its Own Forum Per C&Y, [the contract claim] and [the tort claim] each have a natural forum, but overlapping claims from [the same facts] tilt towards one forum to avoid inconsistent findings.
  • Burden: Legal vs Evidential Per Spiliada and JIO, [defendant] bears the stage 1 legal burden and [claimant] stage 2; evidential burdens shift as each proves [what it asserts]. Some weight goes to jurisdiction founded as of right (C&Y).
  • Appellate Review Per Spiliada and C&Y, precedents help little and the judge's evaluation stands unless [a misdirection in principle / wrong factors weighed or ignored / plainly wrong]; so frame [the error on governing law] as one of principle (Rotary).
  • Forum Shopping (±) [Defendant] calls [claimant]'s pursuit of [higher damages / wider discovery] forum shopping. Per Spiliada, [claimant]'s advantage is [defendant]'s disadvantage; per C&Y, only abusive shopping (oppressive, risking inconsistent judgments) offends, policed by FNC, ASIs and choice of law (Goh Suan Hee).

Availability of [X]

  • Legal Availability Per C&Y, [X] is available if its courts have jurisdiction over [defendant] and will exercise it, assessed at the date of [the stay application]. If no forum is available, Singapore is the natural forum by default.
  • Leave Needed Abroad (+) Per Hindocha, [X] is unavailable if jurisdiction there needs [permission to serve out]; cured by [defendant]'s undertaking to submit or evidence that permission [was / would be] granted.
  • Foreign Court Declines (+) If [X's court] stays its own proceedings, [X] is unavailable. Singapore may stay pending [X's jurisdiction ruling], [claimant] applying to lift the stay if [X] declines (Rappo; C&Y).
  • Practical Availability → Stage 2 Per C&Y (following the SGCA), [fair trial / time bar / missing remedy] in [X] go to stage 2, not to availability.
  • Non-Court Forum (−) Per The Rainbow Joy, [a labour tribunal] in [X] suffices if competent over [the dispute], even with narrower remedies, comity being between nations, not courts.
J4.1.2 Stage 1: Connecting Factors

(not a numbers game; write only those live)

  • Quality over Quantity Per Rappo and JIO, the court draws no "balance sheet" of tenuous contacts; only links relevant to [the dispute] count, so [a Singapore bank account / directorships here] are disregarded.
  • Parties: Timing and Domicile Per C&Y, connections are judged at [the time of the events] and of [the suit]. Domicile carries little weight in commerce, and less for [highly mobile / multi-jurisdiction parties] (Mulacek).
  • Parties: Neutral or Counterbalancing [Claimant] in [Singapore] and [defendant] in [X] cancel out; residence in neither forum is neutral. A company is where it actually does business, e.g., [its Jakarta office] (JIO).
  • Internal Management (±) Per Oro Negro, [the place of incorporation] is prima facie the forum for [corporate governance disputes], here [Singapore / X].
  • Foreign NEJC (No Breach) Per Shanghai Turbo, [clause X] shows only that [X] is an appropriate forum, so it carries little weight; a breached clause engages strong cause instead (J4.3).
  • Singapore Clause Without a Promise (+) Per Chong's answer scheme, where [clause X] naming [Singapore] contains no promise under [law X], Spiliada applies, but the clause still points to Singapore at stage 1.
  • Witness Convenience (+) Per JIO, location carries little weight given [video-link], less still where [X] is proximate like [Malaysia / Indonesia] (Chan Chin Cheung).
  • Witness Compellability (−) Per JIO, non-party [witnesses in X] not compellable here point to [X], if [defendant] shows their evidence is "at least arguably relevant" to its defence.
  • Compellability Reduced (+) Weight falls if [witnesses] will [attend / testify by video] (Raffles Education), or are [defendant's own employees], not third parties (Chong's feedback); it is neutral if the problem arises in both fora (C&Y).
  • Defence Not Pre-Judged Per C&Y, the court must not be claimant-centric and may infer [defendant]'s likely defence from [the statement of claim], but a reticent [defendant] cannot complain.
  • Documents Per C&Y, location is neutral, but [language / translation / admissibility] and [disclosure available only in X] may tip it.
  • Events: Contract Per C&Y and Rotary, [the place of contracting / performance] is neutral unless [formation / performance] is disputed, and fortuitous links get little weight.
  • Events: Tort (−) Per JIO and The Albaforth, the place of the tort, located by the substance test (C3), is prima facie the natural forum unless fortuitous, here [X] because [the representation was received and relied on there].
  • Tort: Starting Point Only Per The Peng Yan, the place of the tort is only a starting point; per Oro Negro and Chen v BHNV, a tort [parasitic on / intimately connected with] [the contract claim] follows that claim's forum.
  • Governing Law: When Weighty Per Rickshaw and JIO, a prima facie view of [law X] suffices. It weighs where [the dispute] turns on law, not on fact ([what was said]). Characterise first: [the contract] and [the tort] follow their own rules (C2; C3).
  • Stage 1 Is Not the OPL Test Per Chong's feedback, do not conflate the two: [connections to the contract] overlap, but [witness compellability] and [where the patent is registered] go to Spiliada only.
  • Governing Law: More Weight (−) Per Rotary, [Saudi law] governing [the JVA] was "a weighty factor". Weight rises with [an express choice of law] (Rappo) or [a trust] (C&Y), and with very different systems, e.g., [Swiss law, which has no trust doctrine] (Ivanishvili v Credit Suisse).
  • Governing Law: Less Weight (+) Weight falls where [law X] is similar to Singapore's, unpleaded (though the court may notice it likely differs: Rickshaw; JIO), referable to [a foreign court] (ROC O 29 / O 29A), or transferable to the SICC (J4.4).
  • Governing Law: Split or Different Choice Per C&Y, where [the claims] are governed by [different laws], or [X's court] would apply [a different law], the factor is reduced or neutral (J4.2.2).
  • Framing the Claim (+) Per Rickshaw, absent bad faith [claimant] may plead [tort] to sidestep [the German choice of law clause], shaping the litigation (C&Y).
  • Singapore Statute Per JIO, [the Misrepresentation Act] points to Singapore only if Singapore law governs [the contract]; here, with [law X] governing, it does not.
  • Related Proceedings: Weight Per Lakshmi and C&Y, overlapping proceedings in [X] matter for the risk of inconsistent judgments (then duplication), weighed by [progress], parties and issues, not by who sued first (cf the EU first-seised rule).
  • Related Proceedings: Disregarded Proceedings [unrelated / infected by the same breach] are irrelevant (Oro Negro). Parallelism [claimant] created by suing in both is disregarded (IM Skaugen (CA)), as is a tactically manufactured link (C&Y).
  • Undertaking to Discontinue (+) Per C&Y, [claimant], having sued in [Singapore and X], may undertake to discontinue in [X] to neutralise [the related proceedings] factor.
  • Shape of the Litigation (±) Per C&Y and Mulacek, staying [the claim] would [fragment / consolidate] the dispute with [the co-defendants], the risk being greatest in multi-party cases.
  • Cambridgeshire Factor (−) Per Spiliada, expertise built up in [X] through related litigation over [the casualty] favours [X], but only in complex technical cases and not on mere assertion (C&Y).
  • SICC Factor (±) Relevant only if [claimant] names the SICC feature suiting [the dispute] and transfer is prima facie available (Rappo); never decisive alone (IM Skaugen) (J4.4).
J4.1.3 Stage 2: Substantial Justice

(justice balanced against comity; all circumstances count)

  • Threshold (−) Per Rotary, once [X] is clearly more appropriate, refusing a stay is "at least unusual"; [claimant] needs cogent evidence of a real and material risk of injustice in [X].
  • Real Risk, Cumulatively (+) Per AK Investment, a real risk in [this case] suffices, not proof that justice "will not" be done, and [several irregularities] may add up though none suffices alone (C&Y).
  • Comity (must write) (−) Per The Hung Vuong-2 and C&Y, the court is slow to impugn [X]'s courts, especially a well-established system (Shen Sophie: [US / China]), so generalised complaints about [X] fail.
  • Ordinary Differences (−) Per C&Y, ordinary differences in [procedure / remedies] are disregarded. Per Spiliada and Trendtex, [higher damages / discovery] here do not defeat a stay if substantial justice is available in [X].
  • Higher Damages (−) Presently [claimant] would recover [one third] of the Singapore award in [X]. Per Chong's feedback, a legitimate juridical advantage alone is not conclusive; per Goh Suan Hee, a likely higher award here was neutral, quantum's governing law being open.
  • Relief Unavailable (−) Per Rappo, [no constructive trust / tracing] in [X] is no injustice where [law X] governs, as it would be unavailable here too; per The Rainbow Joy, narrower remedies are not decisive.
  • Foreign Public Policy (±) Per Trisuryo, [X]'s public policy barring [the claim] is relevant but not determinative, rejecting the view that it is "a strong factor in favour of a stay".
  • Security Here (+) Per Spiliada, [claimant] keeps [its security] under a conditional stay.
  • Delay (−) Presently trial takes [5 years] in [X] against [10 months] here. Per C&Y, delay is disregarded unless egregious; per Grains and Industrial (and Chong's feedback), even alleged delays of [over 10 years in India] failed.
  • Funding and Legal Aid (±) Per Connelly v RTZ, [claimant] takes [X] as it finds it, so [no legal aid] alone fails, unless [the dispute] cannot be justly tried without [lawyers and experts] it cannot fund (Lubbe v Cape).
    • Illustration (+): per Chong (seminar), [no funding] counts only if [claimant] cannot pursue the claim in [X] at all, Singapore being less generous on legal aid. Similar to Limbu v Dyson (EWCA), where migrant workers at Malaysian factories, unable to fund suit there, sued the parent in England, [claimant] cannot fund [the action] in [X].
  • Time Bar (±) Per Spiliada, if [claimant] reasonably sued here and did not unreasonably let time run in [X], the stay is conditional on [defendant] waiving the bar. Presently [claimant] [was unaware / knowingly let time run], so the stay is [conditional / unconditional].
  • Time Bar: Limits (−) Per Chong's feedback, assert [claimant] is out of time in [X] only if the facts say so; parties often file [protective writs] in several fora. If only [some claims] are barred, the court is less willing to keep the case (C&Y; FLPA: J4.2.4).
  • Unfair Rules of Evidence (±) Per Rotary, system-wide rules like [discounting non-Muslim / female witnesses] can in principle ground injustice, but only if they prejudice [claimant] on the facts; cogent evidence is needed (Best Soar).
  • Instability (−) Per Shafeeg, [political unrest] in [X] is insufficient while its courts still function to try [the dispute].
  • Unfair Trial or Danger (+) [Cumulative irregularities] (AK Investment) or [risks of assassination / trumped-up charges] (Deripaska) suffice on cogent evidence, as does a system that "flies in the face of all accepted principles of justice" (Q & M v Poh Kiat).
  • No Trial Anywhere (+) Per AK Investment (applying Connelly v RTZ), where trial in [X] "never could" occur, justice favours Singapore; investing in [X] does not bind [claimant] to its courts.
  • Stay Is Suspensory (±) Per Rotary, where evidence of [arrest / danger] is thin, a stay goes, but [claimant] may lift it on fresh cogent evidence of exceptional circumstances striking at its basis, not as "a standing invitation".
  • Defendant's Stage 2 (±) If stage 1 fails, [defendant] may in principle show injustice in Singapore (IM Skaugen (HC)), e.g., [no legal aid or contingency funding for a foreign defendant], but it rarely succeeds (C&Y).
J4.1.4 Evaluation: Spiliada vs the Australian "Clearly Inappropriate Forum" Test

(where [claimant] argues Singapore is not clearly inappropriate)

  • The Australian Test Per Voth, an Australian court stays only if it is "a clearly inappropriate forum", asking whether local trial is [oppressive or vexatious] rather than whether [X] is more appropriate; reaffirmed in Puttick v Tenon.
  • Singapore's Rejection Per JIO and Eng Liat Kiang, [defendant] must show [X] clearly more appropriate, a test that stays [the dispute] more readily even where Singapore could try it.
  • Comity Per C&Y, Spiliada respects [X] as natural forum and is more consonant with comity at stage 1; but its stage 2 judges [X]'s courts, which Voth avoids, so [defendant] should confine comity arguments to stage 1.
  • Outcome-Determinative? The tests diverge only where [X] is more appropriate but Singapore not clearly inappropriate, so here the choice [is / is not] decisive.
J4.2 FORUM CONVENIENS ON SERVICE OUT: REQUIREMENT (B) OF J3.1 (BURDEN ON CLAIMANT) (run J4.1.2 and J4.1.3, reversing the burdens as below)
J4.2.1 Burden and Standard
  • Mirror Image (must write) Per Zoom, forum conveniens (service out) and forum non conveniens (stay) are one Spiliada test with the burden reversed: [claimant] shows Singapore is more appropriate; on a stay, [defendant] shows [X] is. It goes only to PD para 63(2)(b), not a free-standing discretion.
  • Burden Stays on Claimant Per Spiliada, Oriental Insurance and Zoom, [claimant] bears it without notice and still when [defendant] sets aside, jurisdiction over [a foreign defendant] being "an imposition" on it (Li Shengwu).
  • Not Merely Equal (−) [Defendant] argues Singapore is at most one of several equal fora. Per Sudarga, that fails: [claimant] must show Singapore is the more appropriate forum, with the most real and substantial connection (Oro Negro).
  • How Much More (±) Per Spiliada, AK Investment and Zoom, [claimant] must show Singapore "clearly" more appropriate than [X]; per Oro Negro (adopting Siemens), "on balance" suffices, "by a hair or by a mile". Cite "clearly", flag Oro Negro, argue [both].
  • Asymmetry Deliberate On a stay, [defendant] must show [X] clearly or distinctly more appropriate (JIO; CIMB), protecting jurisdiction as of right. JIO's relativity answers [defendant]'s better-forum point; it does not let [claimant] rest on parity.
  • Quality Not Quantity Per Oro Negro (following JIO and Rappo), [claimant] cannot win by counting [connecting factors]; their weight against [the issues in dispute] decides.
J4.2.2 Stage 1: Centre of Gravity

(run J4.1.2 with the burden on [claimant])

  • Choice of Law Does Double Duty The governing law found under [C2 / C3] for gateways (e) and (f) is also a stage 1 factor (J3.1.2); per Shen Sophie, double actionability applies at service out because [the place of the tort] is needed for the natural forum anyway.

J4.2.3 Stage 2 After Service Out
  • Claimant Wins Stage 1 (±) Per IM Skaugen (HC), [defendant] may still show it would suffer injustice here. Confine this to [procedural differences, e.g., broader disclosure in X], not aspersions on Singapore's courts.
  • Merits No Answer (+) That [defendant] would win in [X] but lose here goes to the merits. Per Vinmar and Chong (seminar), the court does not engage with the merits at the jurisdictional stage, so [claimant] will probably keep [the case].
  • Claimant Fails Stage 1: Second Bite? (must write) Presently [the factors point to X], so can [claimant] still show injustice there? Per Oro Negro and IM Skaugen (CA), this is open: Lewis v King and Metall for; Konamaneni and Fentiman against.
  • Against (−) Per Konamaneni, [claimant] failing stage 1 "is the end of the matter"; per Allenger, running stage 2 would confer jurisdiction over [defendant] where none was established.
  • For: Spiliada Itself (+) Spiliada was a service-out case: England was not the natural forum, yet leave was granted because [the claim] was time-barred in [Canada]. Lord Goff also weighed [defendant]'s residence and [the gateway invoked].
  • For: Principle (+) Per Shen Sophie (adopting Yeo) and C&Y, access to justice and even-handed treatment of [claimant] and [defendant] apply both ways, both bases rest on statute, and O 8 was meant to expand [extraterritorial jurisdiction].
  • Konamaneni's Fallback (+) Even on the narrow view, injustice in [X] may make it not "available" at stage 1, though the SGCA prefers [fair-trial points] at stage 2. On Spiliada's framing, "appropriate forum" is the conclusion after stage 2.
  • Substance (±) Run J4.1.3. E.g., [an allegation that X's courts favour state-linked parties like defendant] needs cogent evidence (AK Investment) and is risky: if it fails, [the case] goes to [X] with the allegation on record.
J4.2.4 Time Bars and the FLPA
  • Common Law Per C&Y, a foreign rule extinguishing [the right] was substantive and one barring [the remedy] procedural, so [claimant] could be out of time in [X] but in time here, a classic stage 2 argument.
  • FLPA Per FLPA s 3(1), where [law X] governs, its limitation law applies and Singapore's does not; per s 3(2), in tort [the lex loci delicti]'s period applies, or that of [the single law] chosen under the flexible exception (C3).
  • Public Policy Per FLPA s 4(2) and Recovery Vehicle 1, [X]'s period is disapplied only for "undue hardship" caused by the bar itself, weighing [its reasonableness], [the claim's complexity] and [claimant's reason for letting time run].
  • Effect If every court applies [the lex causae]'s period, the bar follows [claimant] everywhere, so time bars recede at stage 2, surviving only where [the two courts' choice of law rules] differ (C&Y).
J4.2.5 Parallel Proceedings and Strategy
  • No First-Seised Rule Unlike Brussels (the "Italian torpedo"), each court decides for itself, so both may keep [the case], risking [duplication] and [conflicting judgments].
  • No Estoppel From X's Ruling (±) Per Lakshmi, [X]'s ruling that it is the natural forum does not estop Singapore, though it may bear on comity.
  • Use [The proceedings in X] are a stage 1 factor weighed by progress and overlap (J4.1.2), and may support [a limited stay] or [forum election] (J4.5).
  • No Separate Stay Application Per Zoom, [defendant] should argue forum within its set-aside, where [claimant] bears the burden (the "obvious advantage": C&Y). A separate stay on the same material is "wholly unnecessary and likely counter-productive".
  • Fall-Back Stay Not Submission Per Zoom and O 6 rr 7(6), 12(5), [a fall-back stay] is not submission (J2.2.3). But per Zoom, running it takes on the stage 1 burden [defendant] could have left with [claimant].
  • Practice Tip Advise [claimant] to serve in where possible: served in, [defendant] bears stage 1 (J4.1); served out, [claimant] bears it throughout (J3.0.2).
J4.3 STRONG CAUSE
J4.3.0 Starter (must write)

Presently, [claimant] sues here in breach of [the EJC for X] / [defendant] seeks a stay or set-aside in breach of [the Singapore EJC / NEJC]. Per Vinmar (restating The Eleftheria), the court holds the parties to their bargain unless [the party in breach] shows strong cause, whether on a stay or a set-aside (Shanghai Turbo).

J4.3.1 Nature of the Test
  • Not Spiliada (must write) Strong cause applies where [a party] breaches [clause X]; Spiliada where [no one] does. Never call Spiliada "the strong cause test".
  • Higher Hurdle Per Vinmar, the test rests on party autonomy and commercial certainty, so "it should take more" to keep [the case] from [the chosen court] than on FNC, the court being asked to sanction [a breach of clause X].
  • Foreseeable Factors Per Vinmar, [witnesses in X / foreign law] foreseeable at contracting carry little weight, and parties are deemed to know [the chosen court's procedure / delays] (Shanghai Turbo).
  • FNC Waiver Per Bunge, with [an express FNC waiver] in [clause X], grounds founded on convenience cannot amount to strong cause (J1.1).
  • Singapore NEJC Per Shanghai Turbo, strong cause means the same for [a Singapore NEJC], [defendant] equally reneging on its promise; [witnesses, documents and residence in China], with [related Chinese proceedings], were insufficient there.
  • Two Grounds Per Vinmar, where only [the clause's parties] are involved, strong cause is generally [abuse of process] or [denial of justice]; with [non-parties], fragmentation may suffice (J4.3.4). The list is not closed.
J4.3.2 The Eleftheria Factors (as restated in Vinmar)
  • (a)–(c) Connections Where [the evidence] is and the relative cost of trial in [Singapore / X]; whether [law X] applies and differs materially; how closely [each party] is connected with [X]. Usually foreseeable, so rarely enough alone.
  • (d) Abuse; (e) Prejudice (d) whether [the applicant] is acting abusively; (e) whether [claimant] would be prejudiced in [X] by losing [security], being unable to [enforce], facing [a time bar], or getting no fair trial for [political / racial / religious] reasons.
  • Flip for a Singapore Clause Framed for a suit here in breach of [a foreign EJC]. Where [defendant] objects to [a Singapore EJC / NEJC], keep (a)–(c) but flip (d) and (e): [claimant]'s seising is abusive, or [defendant] would be prejudiced defending here (Shanghai Turbo; Vinmar).
  • Caution on Flipped (e) Like a defendant's Spiliada stage 2, this tells a Singapore judge Singapore is unjust, so confine it to [concrete, cogently evidenced prejudice] to [defendant].
  • Foreseeable Prejudice Per Chong's mid-term comments, that [the claim] is time-barred in [X] but alive under [Singapore's longer period] is arguably no prejudice to [defendant], which agreed to Singapore's jurisdiction and so foresaw its time limits.

1Start here: J4 in plain English

J2 and J3 asked whether the court has power over the defendant. J4 asks whether it should use that power, or send the parties to another country's court. There are only two tests, and the clause decides which:

SituationTestBurden on
Someone broke a jurisdiction clauseStrong cause (Vinmar, restating The Eleftheria)The party in breach

(Two exceptions: in a CCAA case there is no discretion at all (J1.3, J1.4); in the SICC with a written agreement, O 2 r 3 replaces both tests (J5.3).)

★ IN PLAIN ENGLISH

Spiliada is like choosing the best venue for a match when nobody booked one: which stadium has the most real connection with the teams and the fans? Strong cause is different: the teams booked a stadium in advance (the clause). If one team now wants to play elsewhere, it has broken the booking, so it needs a very good reason. "It's a bit far for our fans" is not good enough: they knew where the stadium was when they booked it.

  • Meaning. Forum non conveniens means "not the appropriate forum", not mere convenience such as flight costs (Spiliada).
  • Comparative, not abstract. The defendant must show the foreign forum is clearly more appropriate; that Singapore has few links is not enough (JIO). If the factors are scattered across several countries or finely balanced, no stay.

ℹ NOTE
J4.1.4 Evaluation: Spiliada v the Australian test

Australia stays only if it is a "clearly inappropriate forum" (Voth; Puttick v Tenon). Singapore rejected this (JIO; Eng Liat Kiang). ▤ J4.2.4

✎ WORKED EXAMPLE
Why the FLPA shrinks the time-bar argument

A contract is governed by Ruritanian law, which gives two years to sue. Singapore's own period would be longer. Alpha waits three years, then sues in Singapore and argues: "If you stay this case, I'm out of time in Ruritania, so I'd suffer injustice."

Under the FLPA, the Singapore court would also apply the Ruritanian two-year period, because Ruritanian law governs (s 3(1)). Alpha is out of time here too. Staying the case takes nothing away from Alpha, so there is no stage 2 injustice. Only "undue hardship" caused by the bar itself could disapply the foreign period (s 4(2); Recovery Vehicle 1).

ℹ NOTE
J4.2.5 Parallel proceedings and strategy

There is no first-seised rule (unlike Brussels' "Italian torpedo"): each court decides for itself. A foreign ruling that it is the natural forum does not estop Singapore (Lakshmi). A defendant served out should argue forum within its set-aside, where the claimant bears the burden; a separate stay on the same material is "wholly unnecessary and likely counter-productive" (Zoom).

5J4.3 Strong cause

▤ On your pre-write: J4.3.0 Starter (must write) (word for word)
J4.3.0 Starter (must write)

Presently, [claimant] sues here in breach of [the EJC for X] / [defendant] seeks a stay or set-aside in breach of [the Singapore EJC / NEJC]. Per Vinmar (restating The Eleftheria), the court holds the parties to their bargain unless [the party in breach] shows strong cause, whether on a stay or a set-aside (Shanghai Turbo).

🎓 WHAT PROF CHONG SAID

Never call Spiliada "the strong cause test". Strong cause applies only where someone broke the clause; Spiliada where no one did.

★ IN PLAIN ENGLISH

Spiliada asks "where is the best place for this case?" Strong cause asks "is there a strong enough reason to let you break your word?" Those are very different questions. If you booked a venue a year ago, "it's raining today" or "my friends live far away" are not good reasons to cancel: you knew the weather and the distance when you booked. Only something you could not have foreseen, or something that makes the booked venue truly useless, will do.

J4.3.1 Why it is harder than Spiliada

  • Party autonomy and certainty. "It should take more" to keep the case from the chosen court, since the court is asked to sanction a breach (Vinmar).
  • Foreseeable factors (witnesses abroad, foreign law, the chosen court's delays) carry little weight: the parties knew them when they contracted (Vinmar; Shanghai Turbo).
  • Singapore NEJC: strong cause means the same thing (Shanghai Turbo: witnesses, documents and residence in China, plus related Chinese proceedings, were insufficient).
  • Two grounds where only the clause's parties are involved: abuse of process or denial of justice. With non-parties, fragmentation may suffice. The list is not closed.

J4.3.2 The Eleftheria Factors (as restated in Vinmar)

Flip (d) and (e) for a Singapore clause

The factors were framed for a suit here in breach of a foreign clause. Where the defendant objects to a Singapore clause, keep (a)–(c) but flip (d) and (e): is the claimant's seising abusive, or would the defendant be prejudiced defending here? ✎ WORKED EXAMPLE
Strong cause on a claimant who sued in breach

A clause gives the Tokyo courts exclusive jurisdiction. Alpha sues Nippon KK in Singapore anyway. Nippon seeks a stay, so Alpha must show strong cause. Run the Eleftheria factors:

  1. (a) Evidence and cost: Alpha's witnesses are in Singapore. Foreseeable when Alpha signed: little weight.
  2. (b) Law: Singapore law governs, so a Tokyo court would need it proved. Foreseeable: little weight.
  3. (c) Connections: Alpha is a Singapore company. Foreseeable.
  4. (d) Abuse: Nippon does not admit the debt, so this is not the "admits liability but cannot pay" case. Alpha's real point is that Nippon "has no genuine defence", but per Vinmar the merits are irrelevant.
  5. (e) Prejudice abroad: Alpha says it is now time-barred in Japan. That counts only if Alpha had very good reasons for not filing a protective claim there; otherwise the problem is self-induced.

Result: Alpha is held to its bargain, and the Singapore action is stayed. Compare the same facts under Spiliada: Alpha's points might have carried real weight, because there would be no promise to hold it to.

J4.3.4 Fragmentation and Standard Forms
  • Fragmentation (±) Per Vinmar (citing Donohue), where [non-parties] are involved, splitting [the dispute] across [Singapore and X] risks duplication, inconsistent findings and a race to judgment, and may be strong cause, unless foreseeable at contracting.

✎ WORKED EXAMPLE
When fragmentation might be enough

A Hong Kong EJC binds only Alpha and Beta. Alpha's loss was caused jointly by Beta and by two Singapore companies, C and D, which signed nothing. Alpha sues all three in Singapore. Beta seeks a stay.

If Beta's part goes to Hong Kong while C and D are sued here, the same facts are tried twice, with a risk of inconsistent findings and a race to judgment. If Alpha could not have foreseen C and D's involvement when it agreed to the clause, that fragmentation may be strong cause (Vinmar, citing Donohue).

But if the related foreign proceedings simply involve different parties, causes or remedies, that is not enough (Shanghai Turbo). And in a CCAA case, the same argument fails s 12(1)(c) outright (6DM).

★ IN PLAIN ENGLISH

Normally, "this dispute is governed by German law" pulls towards Germany, because German judges know German law and a Singapore court would need experts to prove it.
