Existence when the defendant is in Singapore: service in and submission

⚡ BIG IDEA

The court has jurisdiction as of right over a defendant served in Singapore or who submits. For every route, name two things together: the founding provision (which part of s 16 SCJA) and the mode of service (which rule). Only a never-registered foreign company needs the Adams v Cape presence test.

J2.0.2 Preliminary Concepts and Statutory Framework

(short opener; subject-matter limits are not examinable, so one line at most)

  • Three Sets of Rules jurisdiction may arise in the [General Division (here)], the [SICC (J5)] or under the [CCAA (J1.2–J1.4)]. Presently [there is no jurisdiction agreement / the [Singapore / X] clause is [within / outside] the CCAA] (router: J1.0.2).
  • Existence Before Exercise per C&Y, existence comes first, so [presence on a fleeting visit] founds jurisdiction and [the weak connection] goes only to a stay (J4.1). Presence and submission are as of right, but [O 7 r 4 service on an agent] and [O 8 r 1(1) service out] need permission.
  • Statutory Source: s 16 SCJA Per Art 93 Constitution and Allenger, the General Division is a creature of statute, so [defendant] must be served [in Singapore] (s 16(1)(a)(i)), served [outside Singapore] as the Rules authorise (s 16(1)(a)(ii)), or [submit] (s 16(1)(b)), or fall within other written law (ss 16(2), 17(1)(c), e.g., ss 387, 376 CA).
  • Service Founds Jurisdiction service both gives [defendant] notice and founds jurisdiction, so even [a submitting defendant] must be served (O 6 r 4), [personally / in an agreed manner under O 7 r 2(1)(d)].
  • Founding Provision + Mode of Service (must write) state [s 16(1)(a)(i) / ss 16(2), 17(1)(c) / s 16(1)(a)(ii) / s 16(1)(b)] with [O 7 r 2(1)(a) / r 2(1)(b) / r 4 / s 387 CA / s 376 CA / O 8 r 1]. Only s 16(1)(a)(i) needs Adams v Cape presence for [the foreign company] (J2.1.5).
  • Why Serve In per Zoom, after service in [defendant] bears the burden on a stay, whereas on service out [claimant] bears it if [defendant] challenges existence on forum grounds (J4.0.2).

  • Jurisdiction vs Power jurisdiction (ss 16–17) is authority to hear [the claim]. Per Li Shengwu, s 18 and Sch 1 concern [powers and reliefs], not [subject-matter jurisdiction], e.g., a stay under [para 9] (J4.1).
  • Inherent Power to Prevent Abuse Per Emilia Shipping, it is arguable that the court's inherent power to prevent abuse, e.g., where [defendant] was [fraudulently lured here] or [property was deceptively brought here], is unaffected by s 16 (now O 3 r 2(2)).
J2.1 EXISTENCE OF JURISDICTION: SERVICE IN SINGAPORE (s 16(1)(a)(i) SCJA)
J2.1.1 Natural Person Physically Present

(an individual in Singapore, however briefly)

  • Basis and Mode Per s 16(1)(a)(i) SCJA, [defendant] present [at his hotel / at the trade fair] is personally served as of right under O 7 r 2(1)(a). Presence is judged at service, so it is immaterial that he [arrived that morning / left the next day].
  • Temporary Presence (+) Per Maharanee of Baroda v Wildenstein and Colt Industries v Sarlie, presence on [a short visit / in transit] suffices without any substantive connection, even if [the contract / accident] arose wholly in [X].
  • Fraudulently Induced Presence (−) Per Watkins v North American Land, service may be set aside for abuse of process where [claimant] lured [defendant] here by [a sham meeting], but not where he came for [business / holiday].
  • Fortuity Goes to Exercise (±) Per C&Y, [defendant]'s weak connection is irrelevant to existence but relevant to the natural forum, so [the fleeting visit] and [the foreign events] support a stay (J4.1).
  • Evaluation: Presence as the Basis (±) [Defendant] calls jurisdiction on [a short visit] arbitrary, preferring [residence / domicile / nationality]. But per Sirdar Gurdyal Singh, jurisdiction is territorial. Presence is also certain where [residence] is harder to prove, and per Chong (seminar), FNC corrects [the fortuity] at the exercise stage.
J2.1.2 Agent of an Absent Natural Person (O 7 r 4)

([defendant] abroad; [agent] manages his affairs here)

  • O 7 r 4 Requirements With permission on a without-notice summons, [agent] may be served for [defendant] if (a) [defendant] does not reside in or is absent from Singapore; (b) at service [agent] personally controls or manages here [defendant]'s affairs specifically relating to [the action]; and (c) [agent]'s authority subsists or there is an ongoing business relationship.
  • Founding Provision and Formalities Per Beranek, O 7 r 4 rests on s 16(1)(a)(i), [defendant] being constructively present through [agent] (J3.0.2). Per C&Y, [claimant] must also post [the order and the claim] to [defendant]'s address abroad within 14 days.
  • Control or Management [Agent] must actually control [the affairs sued on], not merely [relay messages]. Rosenberg's power-to-contract test is framed for companies (J2.1.5), but per C&Y "affairs" reaches [any affairs] of an individual.
  • Exceptional (−) Per C&Y, under the old O 10 r 2 its use against [an overseas individual] was a "rare case", and the English Practice Direction required "great caution", refusing it where [claimant] could easily [serve out].
  • Liberalised (+) Per C&Y, "affairs" replaced "business or work", [O 8] has liberalised service out and [permission] is needed anyway, so O 7 r 4 now applies more readily to [defendant].
  • Service Out Available? (±) [Defendant] argues permission should be refused because [claimant] could serve out under [O 8]. Per C&Y, O 7 r 4 is not meant to make O 8 otiose, but it is unclear that permission should be withheld as a matter of course.
J2.1.3 Company Incorporated in Singapore
  • Basis and Mode Per ss 16(2), 17(1)(c) SCJA, service of [the originating claim] under s 387 CA by [leaving it at / registered post to] [company]'s registered office founds jurisdiction, so Adams v Cape need not be discussed. Per Chong (seminar), in the exam simply state this pathway; the academic argument that [presence / submission] is still needed is not covered.
J2.1.4 Registered Foreign Company (s 376 CA)

(select [company]'s current status)

  • Registrability: ss 365, 368 per s 365, [company] that [establishes a place of business / carries on business] in Singapore, or intends to, must register under s 368 first, lodging [its Singapore registered office] and [its authorised representative].
  • Carrying On Business: s 366 Per s 366(1), business includes [administering property as agent or trustee]. Per s 366(2), it excludes merely [litigating / internal meetings / a bank account / independent-contractor sales / orders accepted abroad / an isolated transaction within 31 days / holding property], so [company]'s [activity] [is / is not] business.
  • Scenario A: Registered and Trading (+) Per ss 16(2), 17(1)(c), [company] is served under s 376(a) ([its Singapore registered office]) or s 376(b) ([its authorised representative's residential or contact address]), without Adams v Cape.
  • Scenario B: Ceased Business, Not Deregistered (+) Per s 376(c), [company] that "has ceased to maintain a place of business in Singapore" is served at [its registered office in X], or under (a)–(b) if [the Singapore office / representative] remains.
  • Scenario C: Ceased and Deregistered (−) Per Tao Commodity, s 376 is unavailable after [deregistration], registration having supplied the nexus, so [claimant] must serve [company] out in [X] (J3.1).
  • Scenario C: Reply (+) But per C&Y, the plain words of s 376(c) cover [the deregistered company], ceasing business being the precursor to deregistration, and the purpose is that [companies that traded here] cannot escape [local creditors]. Tao is a Registry decision unreviewed by the CA.
  • Does s 376 Confer Jurisdiction? (±) Route 1 (C&Y, run this): s 376 itself confers jurisdiction via ss 16(2), 17(1)(c), to spare [local creditors] from [service out]. Route 2 (Tao): registration is "a statutory parallel to or substitute for" corporate presence, s 376 being only [the mode of service]. Both cover Scenarios A–B, but only Route 1 survives [deregistration].
J2.1.5 Never-Registered Foreign Company

(s 376 unavailable; s 16(1)(a)(i) is the founding provision, so Adams v Cape must be written)

  • s 376 Unavailable (must write) although Division 2 applies to [a registrable but unregistered company] (s 365), per C&Y s 376(a)–(b) fail with [no Singapore office or representative] and (c), read holistically, is not meant for [a company still trading here]. So [claimant] relies on s 16(1)(a)(i) and must prove presence.
  • Limb 1: Fixed Place Per Adams v Cape, [company] is present if it carries on business at or from a reasonably permanent fixed place, e.g., [its office at address], whether it [owns / leases / licenses] it. Per Littauer Glove, [a representative taking orders from a hotel for a few days] is not enough.
  • Limb 2: More Than a Minimal Period [company] must be there for more than a minimal period. Per Dunlop v Cudell, [a nine-day trade-fair stand] sufficed, unlike [a one-day visit].
  • Limb 3: Whose Business? where [company] acts through [a representative], it is present only if he carries on [company]'s business, not his own (Okura), shown by [premises acquired for him / fixed pay vs commission / degree of control].
  • Power to Contract (±) Per Adams v Cape, the key question is whether [the representative] can [bind company] without prior approval. Per C&Y, that power is neither exclusive nor conclusive, and [the agent] may also run [its own business] from the same place.
  • Non-Trading Company Per Adams v Cape, where [company] is [a charity / holding company], substitute [its corporate activities] for "business".
  • Mode 1: Officer (O 7 r 2(1)(b), as of right) if [company] is present, [claimant] leaves the claim with [its chairperson / president / secretary / treasurer / other officer]. Per Yeo and Beranek (J3.0.2), it is [company]'s own presence that counts, so serving [an officer on holiday] when [company] is absent is doubtful (contra COSCO; B Atmaram).
  • Mode 2: Agent (O 7 r 4, with permission) Per Rosenberg, [agent] must be obliged to bring [the claim] to [company]'s notice, [company] must carry on business at a fixed place here, and [agent] must have power to contract, otherwise [company] trades "through", not "by", him. Per C&Y, [agent] need not be [a full-time employee].
  • O 7 r 4: Link to the Action (−) Per Rosenberg, the action must relate to [the business agent manages]. Where [the cheques were drawn by the head office in X] and [agent] could do only [liaison work], service is set aside and [claimant] must serve out.
  • O 7 r 4 Narrower Than Adams v Cape Per C&Y, r 4 needs [power to contract], control of affairs specifically relating to [the action], and [permission], none of which Adams v Cape requires. So [claimant] prefers r 2(1)(b) and pleads presence alongside.
  • Unregistrable: No Place of Business (−) where [company] merely [holds a bank account / accepts orders in X] (s 366(2)) or [sells online from X] (no virtual presence: Lucasfilm, by analogy), neither s 376 nor Adams v Cape applies. [Claimant] serves out (J3.1), unless [an agent here] satisfies O 7 r 4 or [Singapore solicitors] accept service (J2.2.2).
J2.1.6 Partnerships and LLPs

(no separate personality, except the LLP)

  • Firm Name (O 56 r 1) [Claimant] may sue [the partnership / limited partnership] in its firm name if [the partners] carry on business here. Per C&Y, this gives no in personam jurisdiction, so each [partner] to be made liable must be served or submit.
  • Mode of Service (O 56 r 3(1)) serve [any partner] or [the person controlling the business at the principal place of business]; if [the firm] is dissolved, serve every partner in Singapore.
  • Sole Trader (O 56 r 9) (−) Per C&Y, r 9 does not reach [a non-resident foreign individual] trading here through [an agent], who would otherwise be worse off than [a foreign partnership], with all his Singapore assets exposed.
  • LLP Per C&Y, [the LLP] is a body corporate served [at its registered office] under LLP Act s 93 (by analogy s 387 CA), which should found jurisdiction since [s 17 SCJA] is not exhaustive.

J2.2 EXISTENCE OF JURISDICTION: SUBMISSION (s 16(1)(b) SCJA)
J2.2.1 Starter

[Claimant] argues [defendant] submitted by [act] under s 16(1)(b) SCJA, usually to cure [irregular service / a jurisdictional objection], though [the originating claim] must still be served (O 6 r 4). Per Allenger, submission was added to s 16 in 1993.

  • Two Levels of Jurisdiction Per Williams & Glyn's, a defendant contesting jurisdiction submits only to [the court's jurisdiction to decide jurisdiction], not to [its jurisdiction over the merits]. So [contesting service / seeking a stay pending X proceedings] while "vigorously protesting" is not submission.
  • The Test Per Zoom (adopting Williams), submission requires a step "only necessary or only useful" if [the objection] had been waived or never entertained. Per C&Y (Spamhaus), it is a clear and unequivocal step incompatible with [no jurisdiction], a question of fact on [defendant]'s conduct as a whole.
  • Scope and Effect Per C&Y, submission cures [irregular service] but cannot confer [subject-matter jurisdiction] (J2.0.2). Per Williams and Glencore, a foreign defendant may limit submission [claim by claim], unlike [a claimant].
  • Submission vs Agreement to Submit Per C&Y, [a Singapore jurisdiction clause] is a promise to submit, enforced by [service out under O 8 r 1(3) or para 63(3)(d)(iv), (r)] (J3) or [strong cause] (J4.3), not submission by conduct.
J2.2.2 Acts Amounting to Submission (+)

(select, and explain why the step is incompatible with objection)

  • Defence on the Merits / Striking Out Per C&Y (Spamhaus), filing [a merits defence] or [a striking-out application] without protest submits, and raising [an EJC in a summary judgment affidavit] is no protest.
  • Stay Sought Alone (±) Per Zoom (The Jian He; Chan Chin Cheung), [an FNC stay] sought without contesting existence ordinarily submits at common law, as [defendant] accepts jurisdiction exists. But per O 6 rr 7(6), 12(5), [a defence or affidavit] stating an exercise challenge is not submission (J2.2.4).
  • Substantive Relief in a Jurisdictional Application Per Zoom (explaining Republic of the Philippines v Maler Foundation), praying for [release of the funds] within [an immunity stay application], pursued whatever [the stay outcome], invokes rather than contests jurisdiction.
  • Participation Despite Reservations Per Shanghai Turbo (SGCA), [deep, active participation in a non-party's injunction application] went beyond [a neutral, pre-emptive procedural step], contradicting [repeated express reservations]: a reservation is "not a silver bullet".
  • Claimant and Counterclaim Per Glencore, [a foreign claimant] suing here submits to "all the incidents of litigation", including [counterclaims] and [an ASI]. Per Lakshmi Anil Salgaocar (HC), filing [a counterclaim] here is likewise premised on jurisdiction.
  • Multi-Party Litigation Per Glencore, [shipowners] who [brought claims and counterclaims] in [multi-party proceedings] submitted for litigation not wholly distinct from [the litigation the ASI protected], despite later [settlement / discontinuance].
  • Instructing Local Solicitors to Accept Service Per Rothschild, [defendant] instructing [Singapore solicitors] to accept service is submission. Per Shanghai Turbo (SGCA) and COSCO (HC), it is service in Singapore under O 7 r 2(1)(d), founding [s 16(1)(a)(i)]; argue both, Yeo preferring submission (YPH lecture: COSCO "should be confined to the submission reasoning") (J3.0.2).
  • Incorporation or Registration (±) [Company] arguably submitted by [incorporation / registration] here. But per C&Y this is unnecessary since [s 387 / s 376] founds jurisdiction via ss 16(2), 17(1)(c), so run it only as [a fallback].
    • Registration (±): (−) per Tao Commodity (HC Registry), registration compelled on pain of [a fine] is not voluntary, so not submission. (+) Per Chong (seminar), [company] chose to do business here, so it arguably submitted.
J2.2.3 Acts Not Amounting to Submission (−)

(then route [claimant] back to J2.1)

  • Notice of Intention to Contest Per O 6 r 6(4), filing [a notice of intention to contest or not contest] within [14 days if served here / 21 days if served out] is not submission or a waiver of [improper service].
  • Defence or Affidavit Stating the Challenge Per O 6 rr 7(4)–(6), 12(3)–(5), [a defence / defendant's affidavit] stating that the court [has no jurisdiction / should not exercise it] is not submission, filed within [21 days if served here / 5 weeks if served out].
  • Notice of Appointment of Solicitor Per Malayan Banking v Zhang Zhencheng, [a notice of appointment of solicitor] filed to challenge jurisdiction is not submission (by parity with O 6 r 12(5)). [Claimant] cannot then serve [those solicitors], unlike in COSCO, where [defendant] agreed.
  • Case Conference Per C&Y, attending [the case conference] after signalling [a challenge] in [its defence] is not submission, the court dealing with jurisdiction first (O 9 r 7).
  • Observer; Defensive Acts Per C&Y, attending [a hearing] as an observer, caveating that [attendance is not submission] (Grains and Industrial Products), or [purely defensive participation, e.g., resisting an injunction to save one's job] (International SOS v Overton), is not submission.

Risk: arguing [the stay] first was "unnecessarily risky" in Zoom, so make clear [existence is still contested].

But this holds only if the court can stay without first assuming jurisdiction, unclear under [Sch 1 para 9 / inherent powers], Singapore lacking [an equivalent of the English statutory power].

1Start here: J2 in plain English

J2 answers the first half of the jurisdiction question: does the Singapore court have power over this defendant at all? (The second half, whether it should use that power, is J4.)

The General Division is a creature of statute. Its power over a defendant comes from s 16 SCJA, which gives three routes:

★ IN PLAIN ENGLISH

Think of the court as a referee who can only blow the whistle on players who are on the pitch. A player standing on the pitch (served in Singapore) is fair game, even if he only walked on a minute ago. A player who walks onto the pitch and starts playing (submits) has accepted the referee. A player in another country can only be brought in with special permission (service out, J3).

✎ WORKED EXAMPLE
Why claimants like service in

Option 1: serve Mr Wong at the conference hotel. Jurisdiction exists as of right (s 16(1)(a)(i), O 7 r 2(1)(a)). If Mr Wong wants the case moved to Hong Kong, he must apply for a stay and show Hong Kong is clearly more appropriate (Spiliada stage 1).

Option 2: wait and serve him in Hong Kong. Alpha needs the court's approval to serve out (J3). If Mr Wong challenges on forum grounds, Alpha must show Singapore is the forum conveniens.

Same dispute, same court. Option 1 puts the uphill work on the defendant; Option 2 puts it on the claimant. That is the practical reason the existence route matters, even though in both cases the court ends up with power.

★ IN PLAIN ENGLISH

"Founding provision + mode" is a door and a key. The founding provision (a part of s 16 SCJA) is the door: it is what lets the court in. The mode of service (an Order 7 rule, or the Companies Act) is the key: it is how you actually open the door. Examiners want both named in one breath, because a key without a door (or a door with no key) gets you nowhere.

✍ EXAM-READY SENTENCES

Individuals: physical presence

🔍 THE PRE-WRITE, IN EVERYDAY WORDS
  • Basis and mode. An individual present in Singapore is personally served as of right: s 16(1)(a)(i) with O 7 r 2(1)(a). Presence is judged at the moment of service, so arriving that morning or leaving next day does not matter.
  • Temporary presence is enough (+). Per Maharanee of Baroda v Wildenstein and Colt Industries v Sarlie, a short visit or transit suffices, without any substantive connection.
  • Fraudulently induced presence (−). Per Watkins v North American Land, service may be set aside as an abuse where the claimant lured the defendant here with a sham meeting, but not where he came for business or a holiday.
  • Fortuity goes to exercise. Per C&Y, the weak connection is irrelevant to existence but supports a stay (J4.1).
  • Evaluation. Is presence arbitrary? Per Sirdar Gurdyal Singh, jurisdiction is territorial. Presence is also certain where residence is harder to prove, and per Chong (seminar), forum non conveniens corrects the fortuity at the exercise stage.
✎ WORKED EXAMPLE
The trade fair

A German businessman attends a two-day trade fair in Singapore and is handed the originating claim at his hotel. The contract was made and performed in Germany. The court has jurisdiction (s 16(1)(a)(i), O 7 r 2(1)(a); Maharanee). Whether it should hear the case is a separate question: the German connections support a stay under Spiliada.

Change the reason he came. Suppose the claimant invented a "settlement meeting" in Singapore purely to get him here and serve him. Now the presence was fraudulently induced, and per Watkins the service may be set aside as an abuse. The line is: why was he here? A real trip (business, a holiday, a trade fair) is fine; a trap is not.

? WHY IT MATTERS
Why such a thin connection is allowed

It can feel unfair that a two-day visit gives Singapore power over a German contract. The answer is the two-stage design. Existence is kept simple and certain (was he here when served?), because presence is easy to prove and hard to argue about. Fairness is handled at the second stage, where a defendant with no real link to Singapore can ask for a stay (J4.1). Per Chong (seminar), forum non conveniens corrects the fortuity.

✎ WORKED EXAMPLE
The landlord abroad

Mr Lee lives in Jakarta and owns a condominium in Singapore. A Singapore property manager, under a continuing management agreement, finds tenants, signs leases for him and handles complaints. A tenant wants to sue Mr Lee over the lease.

  • (a) Mr Lee does not reside in Singapore. ✓
  • (b) The manager personally manages Mr Lee's affairs relating to this very lease. ✓
  • (c) The management agreement is still running. ✓

So the tenant can apply for permission to serve the manager under O 7 r 4, founded on s 16(1)(a)(i): Mr Lee is treated as present through his agent (Beranek).

Contrast: if the only person in Singapore were Mr Lee's cousin, who forwards his post and passes on messages, limb (b) fails. Relaying messages is not controlling or managing his affairs, so the tenant must serve out (J3).

  • Two Levels of Jurisdiction Per Williams & Glyn's, a defendant contesting jurisdiction submits only to [the court's jurisdiction to decide jurisdiction], not to [its jurisdiction over the merits]. Submission is the defendant accepting the court's jurisdiction by what it does. The key idea comes from Williams & Glyn's: there are two levels of jurisdiction.

★ IN PLAIN ENGLISH

Picture a player who walks onto the pitch only to tell the referee, "You have no right to referee me." That is not joining the game: he has accepted the referee's power only to decide that argument. But if he then starts taking free kicks and arguing about the score, he is playing. Submission is the moment a defendant starts playing on the merits.

  • Two levels. A defendant contesting jurisdiction submits only to the court's jurisdiction to decide jurisdiction, not to its jurisdiction over the merits (Williams & Glyn's).
  • The test. Per Zoom (adopting Williams), submission requires a step "only necessary or only useful" if the objection had been waived or never entertained. Per C&Y (Spamhaus), a clear and unequivocal step incompatible with no jurisdiction, judged on conduct as a whole.
  • Scope. Submission cures irregular service but cannot confer subject-matter jurisdiction. A foreign defendant may limit submission claim by claim (Williams; Glencore).
  • Submission v agreement to submit. A Singapore jurisdiction clause is a promise to submit, enforced by service out or strong cause (J3; J4.3), not submission by conduct.

Walking the timeline
  • Day 15, notice of intention to contest. Not submission: filed within 21 days of service out, it is exactly the protest the rules provide for (O 6 r 6(4)).

ROC 2014 deemed a defendant to submit if its challenge failed. ROC 2021 dropped this, so the effect of a failed challenge is unclear.

★ IN PLAIN ENGLISH

Under the old rules, losing a jurisdiction challenge automatically counted as joining the game. The new rules deleted that sentence and did not say what replaces it. So when a defendant who only asked for a stay (accepting the court has power, but saying "send it elsewhere") loses, there are two readings. The broad reading says the rules protect it throughout, so no submission. The narrow reading says the rules protected only the act of filing, so once the stay fails, the defendant is a player like any other. In the exam, set out both and say which you prefer.

ℹ NOTE
Why it matters abroad

If the claimant later enforces the Singapore judgment in another country, that country's rules decide whether the stay application was submission. Per Henry v Geoprosco, an FNC challenge was submission; but per IGCF v Al Jomaih (PC 2025) it is a protest, not acceptance, as REFJA s 5(3)(a)(i) also provides (J7.1).

Hypo: the Indonesian supplier

  3. Mode: an officer (the company secretary) served as of right under O 7 r 2(1)(b).
