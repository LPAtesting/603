How conflict of laws works, and how to use the router

⚡ BIG IDEA

Every conflict question asks one of three things: can a Singapore court hear this case, which country's law decides it, and will Singapore give effect to a foreign judgment. Question 1 tests the first and third, contract by contract; Question 2 tests the second, issue by issue.

1Start here: the whole subject in one page

Conflict of laws (also called private international law) is what happens when a dispute touches more than one country. A Singapore seller, a German buyer, goods shipped from China, a contract that says "English law applies" and "the courts of Hong Kong have jurisdiction". Which court? Which law? And if a court somewhere else has already ruled, does that ruling count here?

★ IN PLAIN ENGLISH

Think of an international football match. Before anyone kicks a ball you need to settle three things. Which stadium and referee? (jurisdiction: can this court hear the case?) Which rulebook? (choice of law: whose law decides who wins?) Does a result from another league count here? (foreign judgments: will Singapore enforce a ruling made abroad?) Conflict of laws is just those three questions, asked carefully, with rules for each.

Walk the three questions through that one story:

  1. Jurisdiction (Question 1). BuyCo is in Germany, so SellCo must serve it abroad, which needs the court's approval and a link to Singapore (J3). The clause also points to Hong Kong. Does it forbid suing anywhere else (exclusive) or merely allow Hong Kong (non-exclusive)? That one reading decides almost everything that follows (J1.1).
  2. Choice of law (Question 2). Suppose Singapore does hear the case. Whether the goods were defective and what BuyCo owes are contract questions, and the contract chose English law (C2.2). So a Singapore judge decides the case by applying English contract law, which the parties must prove as a fact (C1.6).
  3. Foreign judgments (end of Question 1). Suppose instead BuyCo won a ruling in Hong Kong first, saying it owes nothing. Does that ruling bind SellCo in Singapore? That depends on whether Hong Kong had jurisdiction over SellCo in Singapore's eyes (SellCo agreed to Hong Kong courts in the clause, which can count as submission) and whether the ruling was final (J7).

Keep the three questions apart. Singapore can have jurisdiction and still apply English law. A Hong Kong court can be the "right" court and still apply English law too. The most common beginner's mistake is to blur "which court" with "which law".

📖 WORDS TO KNOW FIRST
WordWhat it means in everyday terms
JurisdictionThe court's power to hear a case against this defendant.
Existence v exerciseExistence: does the court have the power at all? (Was the defendant validly served, or did they submit?) Exercise: even with the power, should it hear the case, or send the parties elsewhere? Two separate questions, always in that order.
Service in / service outHanding the court papers to a defendant in Singapore (power exists automatically) or out of Singapore abroad (needs the court's approval and a link to Singapore).
EJC / NEJCExclusive jurisdiction clause ("only the courts of X") / non-exclusive jurisdiction clause ("the courts of X may hear disputes").
SpiliadaThe test for sending a case elsewhere when no clause is broken: is another court clearly more appropriate?
Strong causeThe test when someone breaks a jurisdiction clause: the court holds them to their bargain unless they show a strong reason not to.
HCCCA / CCAAThe Hague Choice of Court Convention and the Singapore Act that brings it in. Where it applies, it overrides the ordinary rules.
SICCSingapore International Commercial Court: a division of the High Court for international commercial cases, with its own rules.
ASI / AEIAnti-suit injunction: an order stopping a party from suing abroad. Anti-enforcement injunction: an order stopping a party from enforcing a foreign judgment.
Proper lawThe law that governs a contract.
Lex foriThe law of the court hearing the case (for us, Singapore law).
Lex causaeThe law that governs the issue, once choice of law has picked it.
Lex loci delictiThe law of the place where a tort happened.
CharacterisationSorting the issue into a legal category (contract? tort? procedure?) so you know which choice of law rule to use.
★ IN PLAIN ENGLISH
Existence and exercise, with a doctor's clinic

Think of a clinic. Existence is whether the patient is registered there at all: if not, the doctor cannot see them, however good the reason. Exercise is whether, even though the patient is registered, the doctor should refer them to a specialist hospital that is better placed to treat them. A defendant served in Singapore (or one who submits) is "registered": the court has power (J2, J3). Spiliada and strong cause decide whether to "refer" the case to a better-placed foreign court (J4). You never ask about referral until you know the patient is registered.

2How the exam is built

🎓 WHAT PROF CHONG SAID

Two compulsory hypothetical questions. Follow the hypo structure from the seminars. Plan in bullets or a table of connecting factors before typing, cite provisions without copying them out, and remember that justification earns the marks. If a threshold is unclear, assume it is met and carry on. Off-syllabus: how renvoi operates, and subject-matter jurisdiction (except the SICC's).

Prof Chong's March 2026 answer framework runs Q1 one contract at a time, because each contract usually has its own clause, its own parties and its own claims. If you mix contracts together you lose track of who breached what. Q2 has no "contracts" to loop over: it is a list of separate issues (Is the choice of law clause valid? Is the contract illegal? Where was the tort committed?), so you take them one by one.

✎ WORKED EXAMPLE
What "contract by contract" looks like on a hypo

Facts (shortened): Alpha (Singapore) signs two contracts with Beta (Indonesia). The supply contract says "the courts of Singapore shall have exclusive jurisdiction". The guarantee, signed a year later, says "the courts of Jakarta may hear disputes". Beta sues Alpha in Jakarta on both. Alpha sues Beta in Singapore on both. (Assume the Hague Convention does not apply.)

Do not write one blended answer. Write two passes:

Supply contract (Singapore EJC)Guarantee (Jakarta NEJC)
TriageExclusive, Singapore chosenNon-exclusive, foreign court named
Who is in breach?Beta, by suing in Jakarta, and again if it objects to SingaporeNobody: a foreign NEJC forbids nothing
Exercise testStrong cause, burden on BetaSpiliada; the burden depends on service and the application
ASI?Contractual basis availableOnly on the vexation or oppression basis

The same two parties end up under different tests, because their two contracts say different things. That is why the router insists on one contract at a time.

  2. Watch the dates. Three dates decide outcomes: (a) did the Hague Convention enter into force for that State before the contract was made? ✎ WORKED EXAMPLE
Spotting the date trap

A contract dated 1 March 2015 picks "the exclusive jurisdiction of the Singapore courts". The Hague Convention entered into force for Singapore on 1 October 2016. So the CCAA cannot apply to this clause: it was concluded before entry into force (J1.2.2). You go straight to the common law rules.

⚠ TRAP
"Assume" is not decoration

Students skim "Assume that under Ruritanian law the clause is void". That sentence is the examiner telling you which issue carries marks. Ask: when would Ruritanian law be the relevant law? Then argue both outcomes.

🔍 THE PRE-WRITE, IN EVERYDAY WORDS
  1. Clause triage (J1). Read the clause. Is it exclusive or not, for Singapore or for a foreign court? Characterise it under its own proper law. If a foreign court has already ruled the Convention applies, ask whether that ruling binds you as an issue estoppel (J7.3).
  2. Hague Convention case? If yes and Singapore is chosen, Singapore must hear it. If yes and a foreign court is chosen, Singapore must stay unless an exception applies.
  3. Otherwise, the common law. Does the court have jurisdiction (existence: J2 if the defendant was served here, J3 if served abroad)? Should it use it (exercise: J4)? Exercise turns on one question: did anyone break the clause? Break it → strong cause. No break → Spiliada.
  4. SICC (J5). Is there a written agreement to the SICC? If not, can the case be transferred there?
  5. ASI (J6). Does the claimant want an order stopping the other side's foreign lawsuit? Four steps: amenability, sufficient interest, a basis (breach of a clause, or vexation/oppression), and no undue injustice.
🧠 MEMORY HOOK
Clause → Court → Convince → SICC → Stop

Clause (triage), Court has power? (existence), Convince it to use it? (exercise), SICC, Stop the foreign suit (ASI). Then judgments at the very end.

✎ WORKED EXAMPLE
One contract, start to finish (outline)

Facts: a 2020 supply contract between a Singapore seller (S) and a Japanese buyer (B) says "the courts of Singapore shall have exclusive jurisdiction". B sues S in Tokyo. S sues B in Singapore and serves B in Japan.

  1. Clause triage: an EJC for Singapore (J1.1).
  2. HCCCA? Only if Japan and Singapore's dates, internationality and subject matter all fit (J1.2); if it applies, Singapore must hear the case (J1.3).
  3. Common law (if not HCCCA): existence by service out with the court's approval (J3). B objecting to Singapore breaks its promise not to object, so B must show strong cause (J4.3).
  4. SICC: does the clause name the SICC, or can the case be transferred there (J5)?
  5. ASI: B's Tokyo suit breaks the EJC, so S can seek an ASI on the contractual basis, and B must show strong reasons why it should not issue (J6.3.1).

Notice how the clause does most of the work. Because it is a Singapore EJC, B is in breach twice over: once by suing in Tokyo, and again if it objects to Singapore. Every later step builds on that finding.

Flip one fact. Make the clause "the courts of Japan shall have exclusive jurisdiction". Now the picture inverts: S is the party in breach by suing in Singapore, so S must show strong cause to keep its action alive (J4.3), and S has no contractual basis for an ASI against B's Tokyo suit, because B is suing exactly where it promised.

🔍 THE PRE-WRITE, IN EVERYDAY WORDS
  • Which regime? If the foreign court was the chosen court in a Convention case, use the CCAA's recognition rules (J7.5). The CCAA also ousts REFJA but leaves the common law open (s 21 CCAA). REFJA itself is not examinable (Prof Chong said so in the seminar), so give it one line at most.
  • Common law requirements. The foreign court must have had international jurisdiction over the debtor, by the debtor's presence there (for a company, carrying on business there) or by submission (for example, through a jurisdiction clause). The judgment must be final and conclusive and for a definite sum of money.
  • Defences. Public policy (for example where Singapore had granted an ASI), proceedings brought in breach of a dispute-resolution agreement (only if the clause is promissory), fraud, and breach of natural justice.
  • Stopping enforcement abroad. If the debtor wants the creditor stopped from enforcing in other countries, it needs an AEI, which requires exceptional circumstances tied to unconscionability (Sun Travels) (J7.6).
✎ WORKED EXAMPLE
A foreign judgment, start to finish

Facts: A Hong Kong court orders Delta, a Singapore company, to pay HK$2 million to Echo. Delta filed a defence in Hong Kong and fought the case on the merits. Delta has no assets in Hong Kong, so Echo comes to Singapore to collect.

  1. Which regime? No exclusive clause chose Hong Kong in a Convention case, so the CCAA's recognition rules are out. REFJA gets one line at most. Go to the common law (J7.4).
  2. International jurisdiction: Delta fought the case on the merits in Hong Kong. That is submission (J7.1).
  3. Final and conclusive, for a fixed sum: a final money judgment for a definite amount.
  4. Defences: fraud, breach of natural justice, public policy, breach of a jurisdiction clause. Nothing in the facts raises any of them (J7.2).
  5. Result: Echo sues Delta in Singapore on the judgment debt. It does not have to prove the original claim again, and Singapore does not re-check whether Hong Kong got the merits right.

Change one fact. Delta never appeared in Hong Kong, never agreed to Hong Kong courts and does no business there. Now there is no presence and no submission, so the judgment cannot be enforced at common law, however correct it was on the merits.

  • Tort double actionability (C3.1; C3.2); locate the tort, misrepresentation being made / received / relied on (C3.3; C3.3.2); flexible exception (C3.6), including party autonomy via the contract's law (C3.6.5); IP and territorial torts (C3.7.2).
  • Contract clause raised against the tort claim the contract negates an element (C3.8.1); an exemption, limitation or non-reliance clause as a defence (C3.8.2).

  • Remedy, quantum, limitation substance vs procedure (C1.2); remedies (C1.3); damages (C1.4); limitation and the FLPA (C1.5; C3.9).

7How to read a pre-write

Every lesson prints your pre-write file in full at the top (folded) and then shows each slice of it in a fold-out box labelled "On your pre-write". The files share a few conventions:

You seeIt means
[claimant], [clause X]A blank to fill with the facts. Replace it with the party's name, the clause number, the country.
Rule A label naming what the point does: rule, application, counter-argument, trap.
(+) (−) (±)Who the point helps: the party relying on the rule, the other side, or argue both ways.
"Per Vinmar", "per C&Y"The source: a case, the Chong & Yip textbook, a statute, or "Per Chong (seminar)".
J4.3A cross-reference to another part of the pre-writes. Click it here to jump.
Similar to 6DM, where…An analogy sentence: you compare your facts to the case's facts.
✎ WORKED EXAMPLE
Turning a pre-write line into an exam sentence

Pre-write: "Per Vinmar, [the party relying on clause X] must show a good arguable case that it exists and applies to [the dispute]."

Exam: "Per Vinmar, Alpha must show a good arguable case that cl 20 exists and covers its claim for unpaid freight."

What changed: the blank "[the party relying on clause X]" became a name (Alpha), "clause X" became the real clause number, and "[the dispute]" became the actual claim. The rule and the source stayed exactly as written. That is the whole technique: keep the law, swap in the facts.

✎ WORKED EXAMPLE
Reading the symbols on one line

Suppose a line reads: "(±) Witnesses: [non-party witnesses] in [country] point to [country] if their evidence is arguably relevant (JIO); less weight if they will give evidence by video."

  • (±) says this factor can be argued both ways, so write both sides, then decide.
  • Witnesses: is the label. Start your paragraph with it so the marker sees the point at once.
  • The square brackets are blanks: "The port inspectors in Jakarta point to Indonesia…"
  • JIO is the source, cited without a paragraph pinpoint.
