The SICC: written agreements, transfer and joinder

⚡ BIG IDEA

The SICC is a division of the High Court for international commercial cases. A case gets there in three ways: a written jurisdiction agreement (primary jurisdiction), transfer from the General Division ("more appropriate"), or joinder. Once there under a written agreement, Spiliada and strong cause disappear: the SICC declines only if hearing the case is contrary to its international and commercial character or an abuse of process.

J5.1 THE SICC: NATURE AND SUBJECT-MATTER JURISDICTION
  • Nature (write one line) Per s 18A SCJA, the SICC (launched 5 January 2015) is a division of the General Division, so a national court: [its judgment] is a Singapore High Court judgment, enforceable in [State] as such (including under the HCCCA); appeals lie to the Court of Appeal; and jurisdiction over [defendant] still rests on s 16 SCJA.
  • Court, Not Tribunal Unlike [an arbitral tribunal], the SICC can coerce [third party C] into the proceedings (J5.6). Per Parliament, though arbitration-influenced, it is "a new way of thinking about international dispute resolution".
  • Special Features (use where relevant) Presently, [the dispute] turns on [foreign law / foreign evidence rules]. The SICC has international judges, may disapply Singapore evidence rules, including on privilege (s 18K SCJA), and may decide foreign law on submissions instead of proof (s 18L).
  • Confidentiality and Counsel Where [the parties] want [confidentiality / their own foreign counsel], the SICC may allow it (O 16 r 9; s 18M), more readily in "offshore" cases with no substantial connection to Singapore.
  • Standalone Rules Where the SICC Rules are silent on [issue], the SICC may do what is necessary, applying the ROC with modifications or looking to [foreign law / international practice] (O 1 r 11(3)). Per C&Y, the standalone SICC Rules 2021 (from 1 April 2022) stress its independence and international character, including procedural flexibility responsive to international commerce (O 1 r 3).
  • Subject Matter (s 18D) Presently, [claimant]'s [claim] falls within [category]. The SICC hears (1) international and commercial actions the General Division could hear in its original civil jurisdiction (s 18D(1)); (2) IAA proceedings on international commercial arbitration (s 18D(2)); (3) pre-action applications for documents, injunctions or search orders, and committal; and (4) international and commercial corporate insolvency, restructuring and dissolution.
  • IAA Proceedings (±) Where [the application] concerns [an international commercial arbitration], permission is needed to serve out (O 5 r 6), and the SICC may decline under O 2 r 3(2) (C&Y). Per C&Y, "international" follows IAA s 5(2), "commercial" the Model Law art 1(1) note, and [investment / State] arbitrations may be included.
J5.1.1 International and Commercial (O 2 r 1(3))
  • International (+) Per O 2 r 1(3)(a), [the action] is international if [one party's place of business / where a substantial part of the obligations is to be performed / the subject matter's closest connection] is outside Singapore, or all parties named on filing expressly agreed it relates to more than one State. Presently, [defendant]'s place of business is [State].
  • Place of Business Per O 2 r 1(4)–(6), [party]'s place of business is where it carries on business; if [several], the one most closely connected to [the written jurisdiction agreement]; if none, its habitual residence. It is fixed when [the agreement] was concluded (or, on transfer, when [the case] began or as the General Division decides).
  • Commercial (+) Per O 2 r 1(3)(b), [the action] is commercial if its subject matter arises from a commercial relationship, contractual or not, or it is an in personam IP dispute, or all parties expressly agreed so. The parties themselves need not be in a commercial relationship (O 2 r 1(5)). Presently, [claim] arises from [a [distribution / construction / loan] relationship].
  • Presumption (+) Per O 2 r 1(8), as each party submitted under [clause X], [the action] is presumed international and commercial, subject to the SICC's review under O 2 r 3 (C&Y). Per Chong (AY23-24 mid-term feedback), where [the facts] are objectively international and commercial, say so briefly and move on.
  • Rebuttal (−) [Defendant] rebuts the presumption: [both parties trade only in Singapore / all obligations were performed here / the dispute is [a residential tenancy / an individual's guarantee]]. Even where the parties agreed [the case] into scope (O 2 r 1(3)(a)(ii), (b)(iii)), per C&Y the SICC may decline it as contrary to its character (O 2 r 3(2)) (J5.3).

J5.2.2 Exclusive or Not? (s 18F SCJA)
  • Presumption of Exclusivity (+) Per s 18F(1), absent express contrary words in [clause X] (s 18F(2)), [the parties] are taken to have agreed (a) to submit exclusively to the SICC; (b) to carry out [its judgment] without undue delay; and (c) to waive recourse to [foreign courts] against the judgment or its enforcement, so far as validly waivable.
  • When Does s 18F Apply? (route by facts) Per C&Y, route [clause X] by [its governing law]:
    • CCAA case: s 3(2) CCAA deems [clause X] exclusive whether [Singapore / foreign] law governs it, so s 18F need not be argued.
    • Singapore law governs [clause X]: s 18F applies, so [clause X] is exclusive absent [express non-exclusive wording].
    • Non-CCAA case, [foreign law] governing: debated. s 18F governs [clause X] only if it is a forum mandatory rule, as [the party wanting exclusivity] argues and [the party resisting] denies (C1.7). C&Y take no side, noting that Yip and Chong differ.
  • s 18F Is a Forum Mandatory Rule (+) Per Chong (Chong & Yip 2019 article), s 18F applies to [clause X] whatever its governing law: (i) the presumption against extraterritoriality is rebutted for a court set up to hear [international disputes]; (ii) s 18F(1)(a) is inspired by art 3(b) HCCCA (now s 3(2) CCAA); (iii) it keeps [non-CCAA SICC clauses] consistent with [CCAA SICC clauses].
  • s 18F Is Not Mandatory (−) Per Yip (same article), s 18F applies only where Singapore law governs [clause X] (in a CCAA case, s 3(2) CCAA deems exclusivity: C&Y). Party autonomy is an important undergirding value of the SICC (C&Y), and a presumption rebuttable by contrary words is not [an overriding mandatory rule]. So [clause X] is construed under [foreign law], under which it is [non-exclusive].
    • Reply (+): per C&Y, the SICC balances [party autonomy] against [expedience], as O 2 r 2(1) shows.
    • Why it matters: little for exercise, as O 2 r 3 treats [EJCs and NEJCs] alike (J5.3). It matters for breach: per C&Y, suing in [foreign court] breaches only an exclusive SICC clause, founding [an ASI / damages] (J6.3.1.3; J1.7).
J5.2.3 Clauses Naming the High Court or General Division (O 2 r 2)
  • Post-2016 High Court Clause Includes the SICC (+) Presently, [clause X], concluded on [date] (on or after 1 October 2016), names [the Singapore High Court / the General Division]. Per O 2 r 2(1) and s 2(2)–(3) CCAA, it includes the SICC unless [a contrary intention] appears, so [claimant] may file directly in the SICC.
    • Critique (−): Per C&Y, O 2 r 2(1) seems to bar evidence that [the parties] [never contemplated the SICC], contrary to Singapore's contextual interpretation and to party autonomy.
    • Reply (+): Per C&Y, the lost autonomy buys HCCCA enforceability of [the SICC judgment] in [State] and fits the SICC's international template; per Hartley–Dogauchi, "the courts of France" lets French law allocate internally.
    • Specific Reference Needed? (−): Per Chong (sample-final answer scheme), arguably [clause X] needs a more specific reference to the SICC to qualify as a written jurisdiction agreement for it, so [defendant] resists [primary jurisdiction].
  • Pre-2016 Clause (−) Per O 2 r 2(2), [clause X], concluded on [date] before 1 October 2016, "does not of itself" submit to the SICC. Per Chong (sample-final answer scheme), there is no primary jurisdiction, so [claimant] sues in the General Division and seeks [a transfer] (J5.4).
  • SICC-Only Clause (−) Per O 2 r 2(3), [clause X], naming [the SICC] alone, is not "of itself" submission to the General Division, so [claimant] suing there cannot rely on it alone (C&Y).
  • Foreign Law Governs [Clause X]: O 2 r 2(1) Procedural or Substantive? (±) Per the slides, this is open where [foreign law] governs [clause X]:
    • (+) Party wanting the SICC: O 2 r 2(1) is a forum rule on internal allocation and on the scope of submission to a Singapore court, so the lex fori applies though [contract] is governed by [foreign law].
    • (−) Party resisting the SICC: construing [clause X] is substance for its proper law, [foreign law], as party autonomy requires; under it, ["the High Court of Singapore"] excludes the SICC.
    • Likely outcome: Per Chong (seminar), [foreign law] probably has no rule on construing ["the General Division of Singapore"], so it falls back on O 2 r 2(1), and [clause X] includes the SICC unless [it expressly excludes it].
    • If Singapore law governs: per Chong (AY23-24 mid-term feedback), the question does not arise: O 2 r 2(1) applies to [clause X], and absent [express contrary words] s 18F makes it exclusive (C&Y).

J5.3 EXERCISE OF JURISDICTION IN THE SICC (O 2 r 3)
  • The Test (must write) Presently, [defendant] asks the SICC to decline because [the dispute centres on [State] / [State B] law governs]. Per O 2 r 3(2), the SICC may decline only if exercising jurisdiction is contrary to its international and commercial character or an abuse of its process, on its own motion or [on application] (O 2 r 3(4)).
  • Foreign Connections Irrelevant (+) Per O 2 r 3(3), as there is [a written jurisdiction agreement], the SICC must not decline "solely on the ground that the action … is connected to a jurisdiction other than Singapore". Per C&Y and Chong (AY23-24 mid-term feedback), there is no Spiliada balancing, Australian test or strong cause, so [defendant]'s links to [State] count for nothing.
  • Same Test for EJCs and NEJCs (+) Per C&Y, the old O 110 r 8 "appropriateness" wording left room to treat an NEJC differently; the 2021 Rules close that gap, as the SICC Committee intended. So although [clause X] is non-exclusive, [defendant] cannot rely on [State B] being more appropriate.
  • Justification (if asked to evaluate) Per the SICC User Guide (Note 1), connections are ignored because [the parties] agreed in writing to "an international court"; per C&Y this is circular, the SICC not being a true international court. C&Y prefer party autonomy: [commercial parties] may choose [a neutral, specialist forum] and must accept the consequences.
  • Contrary to International and Commercial Character (−) Presently, [the dispute] is in substance [a local dispute between Singapore residents / a consumer-type claim], brought in only by [the parties' agreement] under O 2 r 1(3)(a)(ii) or (b)(iii). Per C&Y, hearing it may be contrary to the SICC's international and commercial character.
  • Objectively International and Commercial (+) Per Chong (AY23-24 mid-term feedback), as [the action] is objectively international and commercial, with [defendant] in [State], there is little ground for the SICC to decline.
  • Abuse of Process (−) Presently, [claimant] filed in the SICC only to [delay [State B] proceedings already well advanced / relitigate [issue] decided in [State B]]. Per O 2 r 3(2), that abuses the SICC's process (cf tactical "wait and see" conduct, J6.3.1.3).
  • Consequence Per O 2 r 3(5)–(6), if the SICC declines, it must transfer [the case] to the General Division where that court has jurisdiction and [all parties] consent (deemed for [a "High Court" clause]); otherwise it may dismiss or stay (J5.2.5).

J5.4.1 Non-CCAA Case (O 2 r 4(1))
  • Requirements (must write) Presently, [party] seeks transfer of [claimant]'s General Division action, or the court acts on its own motion after hearing [the parties] (O 2 r 4(3)). Per O 2 r 4(1), it may transfer if (a) [the action] is international and commercial when the order is made; (b) no prerogative relief is sought; and (c) the SICC is more appropriate.
  • Only Route Per Chong (sample-final answer scheme; AY23-24 mid-term feedback), where [clause X] [predates 1 October 2016 / does not cover [the claim against C]], the SICC has no primary jurisdiction, so [claimant] reaches it only by transfer under the O 2 r 4(1)(c) "more appropriate" test.
  • More Appropriate (+) Per C&Y, relevant factors (the Rules give none) are the international elements and their relevance; the SICC's capability to handle them; [the parties]' level of consent; and connections to Singapore. Presently, [the dispute] is [highly international and commercial / governed by [foreign law], decidable on submissions (s 18L)].
    • Not Complexity: Per Chong (seminar), the test turns on how international and commercial [the dispute] is, not its complexity, which [a General Division judge] can also handle.
    • International Judge: Per Chong (AY23-24 mid-term feedback), [an International Judge from [State]] with expertise in [foreign law] favours the SICC (J4.4).
    • Weak Singapore Link: Per C&Y, a case poorly connected to Singapore is arguably more suited to the SICC, given its international character, so [the dispute]'s [few Singapore links] help [claimant].
    • Consent: Per C&Y, as the General Division may transfer without every party's consent, it should lean towards transfer where [all / most] parties consent, as [defendant] does here.
    • Overlap with Spiliada: Per C&Y, where [defendant] seeks a stay at the jurisdictional stage, most factors other than consent are weighed at Spiliada stage 1, where [the SICC factor] alone cannot displace [State] (IM Skaugen; J4.4).
  • Not More Appropriate (−) [The dispute] is [a straightforward Singapore-law debt claim / largely domestic], and [a General Division judge] can resolve it expeditiously (cf C&Y on SICC → General Division transfers), so no SICC feature makes transfer more appropriate.
J5.4.2 CCAA Case With a "High Court" or "General Division" EJC (O 2 rr 4(4), 4(7), 5(1))
  • Every Party's Consent (must write) Presently, the CCAA applies and [clause X] names [the Singapore High Court / the General Division]. Per O 2 r 4(4), transfer also needs every party's consent. Per C&Y, this exceeds s 11(5) CCAA ("take into account") to protect HCCCA recognition: art 8(5) lets [a foreign court] refuse recognition against [a party] that objected in time.
  • Deemed Consent (+) Per O 2 r 4(7), where [clause X] names the receiving court, each party is treated as consenting. With O 2 r 2(1) (a post-2016 "High Court" clause includes the SICC), consent is doubly deemed (C&Y), so [defendant] is treated as consenting although it [objects].
    • Counter (−): Per C&Y, it is unclear whether deemed consent meets the CCAA threshold. [Defendant] objected in time, so [the SICC judgment] risks refusal under art 8(5) in [State], where its assets are; arguably the General Division should insist on actual consent.
    • Discretion Only: Per s 15(4)(b) CCAA (the art 8(5) equivalent), the risk arises only where [the transfer] was discretionary, not mandated by [internal allocation rules].
  • SICC Expressly Excluded (±) Presently, [clause X] names [the High Court] "excluding the SICC". Per O 2 r 5(1), transfer may be conditional on every party varying [clause X] to name the SICC. Per C&Y, the rule is strictly unnecessary (no transfer without consent anyway) but nudges [the parties] to vary, securing clean HCCCA enforcement.
  • Handout Question Can a "High Court" EJC case started in [the General Division] be transferred without breaching the HCCCA? Yes, per O 2 r 4(4) and (7), if [every party] consents, actually or by deemed consent, subject to the art 8(5) risk where [a party] objected in time.

  • Existence (−) Per O 2 r 4(8)(b)(i), the SICC "must not reconsider whether it has jurisdiction unless there has been a material change in circumstances such that the court may cease to have jurisdiction". Presently, [defendant] [can / cannot] reopen it, as there [is / is no] such change, e.g., [a new party / no valid service on [defendant]].

J5.5 TRANSFER FROM THE SICC TO THE GENERAL DIVISION (O 2 rr 4(5), 5(2))
  • Requirements (+) Presently, [the parties] sued in the SICC under [clause X] but now find [the claims are not truly international / no SICC feature is needed]. Outside O 2 r 3(5), per O 2 r 4(5) a party may apply with all parties' consent if the General Division has jurisdiction (it does: s 18A) and is more appropriate (C&Y).
  • More Appropriate: Low Threshold (+) Per C&Y, as all parties consent, the threshold is low; relevant are whether [the international elements] are germane to [the dispute] and whether [a Singapore judge] can resolve it expeditiously.
  • Not More Appropriate (−) Per Chong (AY23-24 mid-term feedback), where [the action] is objectively international and commercial, as with [defendant] in [State], it is hard to see why the General Division is more appropriate.
  • CCAA SICC-Only Clause (±) Per O 2 r 5(2), where the CCAA applies and [clause X] names only the SICC, transfer may be conditional on every party varying [clause X] to name the General Division (C&Y). Deemed consent applies where [the clause] names the receiving court (O 2 r 4(7)).
J5.6 ROUTE 3: JOINDER OF ADDITIONAL PARTIES (s 18D(3) SCJA; O 10)
  • Starter (must write) Presently, [party] seeks to join [third party C] to [SICC action]. Per s 18D(3)–(4), once the SICC has jurisdiction over an international and commercial action, it may hear claims by or against additional parties (joinder, counterclaims, third- and subsequent-party proceedings) if the action as a whole stays international and commercial; [C]'s claims need not be (C&Y).
  • Key Advantage (+) Per C&Y (citing the SICC Committee Report), this coercive joinder is a key advantage over arbitration: [C], though [a non-signatory], can be bound in [one proceeding].
  • Requirements (+) Per O 10 r 5(1), [C] may be joined if the O 10 joinder requirements are met and the claims by or against [C] seek no prerogative relief and are appropriate for the SICC, which considers its international and commercial character (O 10 r 5(3)).
  • Connected Issues (+) Per O 10 r 6(1), joining [C] is appropriate "so that the Court can resolve all the matters in dispute in the proceedings or any issues that are connected to those matters". Presently, [C]'s role as [guarantor / co-contractor / alleged conspirator] is bound up with [the main dispute].
  • No Common Questions (−) Per Chong (AY23-24 mid-term feedback), joinder fails where [the claim against C] shares no common question of law or fact with [the claim against defendant], e.g., [a pre-contractual tort] against [a post-contractual breach].
  • No Written Agreement Needed (+) Per O 10 r 5(2), [C] need not have submitted under a written jurisdiction agreement, unless [C] is [a State or sovereign], which must have (C&Y).
  • Permission (±) Per O 10 r 7(1), SICC permission is generally required, on application by [an existing party] or [C] itself (O 10 r 7(3)), but not where [C] submitted under a written jurisdiction agreement and the originating application is not yet served (O 10 r 7(2)(a)).
  • Service on [C] in Singapore Per O 5 r 2, [C] in [Singapore] is personally served, founding jurisdiction under s 16 SCJA (apply J2.1 to [C's status]).
  • Service on [C] Abroad (±) Per O 5 r 6(2), [C] in [State] is served without permission if party to a written jurisdiction agreement or [an agreement allowing service out]; otherwise, per O 5 r 6(1), with permission if the SICC "has the jurisdiction" and "it is appropriate". Per C&Y, FNC does not apply, unlike the General Division's test (J3.1).
  • C Resists Joinder (−) Presently, [C] seeks a stay under O 2 r 3(2). Per the slides, O 2 r 3(3) (the foreign-connections bar) protects only parties to [a written jurisdiction agreement]. The User Guide ignores connections because the parties agreed in writing; [C] did not, so the SICC may weigh [C]'s links with [State].
  • Burden Arguably [the party seeking joinder] bears it, as it must show the claims against [C] are "appropriate" for the SICC (O 10 r 5(1)(b)(ii)).

★ IN PLAIN ENGLISH

The SICC is a special international courtroom inside the Singapore High Court building. Two versions of C
  • Version 1: C made the defective components that Beta supplied to Alpha. Whether the components were defective is a common question in both claims. The action as a whole stays international and commercial. Joinder is possible (s 18D(3); O 10), and C needs no written agreement (O 10 r 5(2)). If C resists under O 2 r 3(2), the SICC can weigh C's Vietnamese links, because the r 3(3) bar protects only parties to a written agreement.

This is the SICC's edge over arbitration: an arbitral tribunal could not force C in at all.
